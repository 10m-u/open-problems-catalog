# Literal system-density conjecture: countercase

Eberhard's held thesis, physical pages 8 and 30–31 (printed 3 and 25–26), defines the worst subset guarantee on positive integers and extends Conjecture 1.7.3 to arbitrary homogeneous systems. No hypothesis of a common strictly positive rational solution is stated. Both reviewers read these pages and independently checked the following obstruction.

Take the system `x+y=z`, `x+2y=4z`. Its rational kernel consists of `(-2t,3t,t)`. No tuple of positive integers satisfies it, so every finite positive-integer set is solution-free and its worst subset guarantee is 1.

For every prime p>3, the kernel over F_p has the same form. If B⊂F_p has |B|>2p/3, each of B, (-2)^(-1)B and 3^(-1)B has size |B|, and their intersection has size at least 3|B|−2p>0. A point t in that intersection gives a solution (-2t,3t,t) in B³. Thus the finite-field extremal density is at most 2/3 for every p>3. It cannot tend to the positive-integer guarantee 1.

Each row already has positive and negative coefficients. The single-equation sign convention therefore does not repair the system statement. The literal conjecture is refuted; no differently qualified intended question is numbered. Repeated coordinates are allowed by the actual source definition, including the zero tuple over finite fields.

This source-scope reduction is separate from a literature resolution and from the single-equation Conjecture 1.7.1, whose mixed-sign equation always has a positive rational solution.
