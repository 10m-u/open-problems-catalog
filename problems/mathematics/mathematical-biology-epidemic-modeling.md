## Q372. Can there exist \$p>0\$, a continuous function \$g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\$ with \$g(0…

**Status:** Open · **Kind:** open problem · **Collection** 4

Can there exist \$p>0\$, a continuous function \$g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\$ with \$g(0)=0\$, \$g(p)=p\$, and \$g(s)>s\$ for \$0&lt;s&lt;p\$, and a nonnegative kernel \$k\in L^1(\mathbb R)\$ satisfying \$\int_{\mathbb R}k=1\$ but \$\int_{\mathbb R}|x|k(x)\\,dx=\infty\$, for which \$u(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}g(u(x-y))k(y)\\,dy\$ has a nonconstant measurable solution \$0\le u\le p\$ lacking a limit at at least one of \$+\infty\$ and \$-\infty\$?

**Context.** Discovery path (advisor ascent; student → supervisor):
Filippo Quattrocchi → Jan Maas: https://research-explorer.ista.ac.at/record/20563
Jan Maas → Jan van Neerven: https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/analysis-of-infinite-dimensional-diffusions
Jan van Neerven → Odo Diekmann: https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/29008
Odo Diekmann → Hendrik Adolf Lauwerier; L. A. Peletier (co-promotor): https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/23606/Thesis_O_Diekmann Origin: The thesis contains Diekmann’s joint work with Hans G. Kaper. Remark 4.10 explicitly leaves this question open after identifying the finite first absolute moment as the hypothesis used in their asymptotic-limit lemma. The same question appears in their 1978 journal article, p. 731; it is not attributed to an earlier author.
