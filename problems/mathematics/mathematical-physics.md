**Context.** Origin for questions 1484, 1485, 1486, 1487: SAW: classical predictions. Prudent: thesis-origin conjectures, later joint paper. ## Q1688. Negative-partial-transpose bound entanglement

**Status:** Open · **Kind:** open problem (Problem 4) · **Collection** 17

Does a finite-dimensional bipartite state ρ exist with ρ^Γ not positive semidefinite but ⟨ψ|(ρ^Γ)^⊗n|ψ⟩≥0 for every n≥1 and every Schmidt-rank-at-most-two vector ψ?

**Context.** Origin for question 1688: Distillability question following M., P. and R. Horodecki (1998); source Problem 4. Setup for question 1688: A bipartite state is a positive semidefinite trace-one matrix. Γ transposes its first subsystem. A pure vector has Schmidt rank at most two if it is a sum of at most two product vectors, across A^⊗n:B^⊗n for n copies.

**Source.** Paweł Horodecki, Łukasz Rudnicki, Karol Życzkowski. *Five Open Problems in Quantum Information Theory*. 2022. [primary source](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.3.010101) Location: Problem 4.

## Q1689. Two PPT steps break entanglement

**Status:** Open · **Kind:** conjecture (Conjecture I.1) · **Collection** 17

For every d≥4 and every PPT map T:M_d→M_d, is T∘T entanglement breaking?

**Context.** Origin for questions 1689, 1690: PPT square: Christandl (2012). Schmidt-number bound: Sanpera–Bruß–Lewenstein (2001). Setup for questions 1689, 1690: M_d denotes complex d×d matrices. A map T:M_d→M_d is PPT if T and transpose∘T are completely positive. Its Choi matrix is C_T=Σ\_ij|i⟩⟨j|⊗T(|i⟩⟨j|). Entanglement breaking means C_T is a sum of positive product matrices. Schmidt number is the smallest k admitting a positive sum of pure-state projectors of Schmidt rank at most k.

**Source.** Matthias Christandl, Alexander Müller-Hermes, Michael Wolf. *When Do Composed Maps Become Entanglement Breaking?*. 2019. [primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01266) Location: Conjecture I.1, p. 2.

**Literature check.** Status for questions 1689, 1690, checked 6 October 2026: qutrit, finite-iteration and almost-maximal-Schmidt results leave both general targets open.

## Q1696. Exact quantum depolarizing capacity

**Status:** Open · **Kind:** open problem · **Collection** 17

**Context.** Origin for questions 1696, 1697: Classical open capacity-evaluation problems, restated in the source introduction. Setup for questions 1696, 1697: For a qubit define D_p(ρ)=(1−4p/3)ρ+(4p/3)I/2, with 0&lt;p<1/4 (p is total Pauli-error probability).
