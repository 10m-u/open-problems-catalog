## Q219. Over an algebraically closed field \$K\$ of characteristic zero, let \$H_{d,n}\$ sen…

**Status:** Open · **Kind:** open problem (Question 2.7.1) · **Collection** 3

Over an algebraically closed field \$K\$ of characteristic zero, let \$H_{d,n}\$ send a degree-\$d\$ hypersurface \$X=V(F)\subset\mathbb P^n\$ to the Zariski closure of \$\\{[\operatorname{Hess}F(p)]:p\in X\\}\$ in the projective space of symmetric matrices. Is this rational correspondence birational onto its image for \$d\ge2\$, \$n\ge1\$ and \$(d,n)\ne(3,1)\$?

**Context.** Origin: Hessian reconstruction develops the author’s sole-authored 2023 paper. Game-theoretic varieties are joint with Irem Portakal, preprinted in 2022/2024 and published in 2024/2025.

## Q220. For a graph consisting of two disjoint edges and any number of isolated vertices…

**Status:** Open · **Kind:** open problem (Question 3.6.1) · **Collection** 3

For a graph consisting of two disjoint edges and any number of isolated vertices, is N(X,G) a smooth complex projective surface for generic payoff tables X?

**Context.** Origin: Hessian reconstruction develops the author’s sole-authored 2023 paper. Game-theoretic varieties are joint with Irem Portakal, preprinted in 2022/2024 and published in 2024/2025. Shared setup for questions 220, 221: For a binary n-player game X and an undirected graph G on the players, V(X,G) is the projective complex Zariski closure of tensors p satisfying the global Markov conditional-independence equations of G and equality of each player’s conditional expected payoff between their two actions. Take the closure only after excluding zero tensor coordinates and zero one-player marginal sums. This is the Spohn CI variety. When G is a disjoint union of cliques, it is the Nash CI variety N(X,G). Positive normalized real points are totally mixed CI equilibria.

**Source.** Javier Sendra Arranz. *Algebraic Methods in Reconstruction of Varieties and Game Theory*. University of Tübingen, 2024. Advisor(s): Daniele Agostini. ## Q291. For each integer m ≥ 2, consider this symmetric m-player three-action game. Rock…

**Status:** Solved here: proved · **Kind:** conjecture (Conjecture 5.7.6) · **Collection** 3

For each integer m ≥ 2, consider this symmetric m-player three-action game. Rock wins whenever rock and scissors are both present; otherwise paper wins when both paper and rock are present, and scissors wins when both scissors and paper are present. If everyone chooses the same action, that action wins. With k players choosing the winning action, each receives m/k − 1 and everyone else receives −1. Must every mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium have at least two players assigning positive probability to scissors?

**Context.** Origin: New equilibrium conjecture for the author’s unfair multiplayer rock–paper–scissors game; its algebraic reformulation is counted once.

**Source.** Itai Maimon. *Homological Codes, their Extensions, and Certain Unfair Games*. University of California, San Diego, 2024. Advisor(s): David Meyer. [primary source](https://escholarship.org/content/qt84c9t3c3/qt84c9t3c3.pdf) · [record](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/84c9t3c3) Location: §5.7, Definition 5.7.3, Theorem 5.7.5 and discussion, pp. 159–160; equivalent infeasibility Conjecture 5.7.6 on p. 162.

**Further links.** [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13736) · [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.13736)

**Result (2026-10-09).** Every mixed Nash equilibrium of the source three-action imbalanced rock-paper-scissors game has at least two players assigning positive probability to scissors, for every integer m>=2. Exact three-action game; no claim for the source's larger blown-up action spaces.
