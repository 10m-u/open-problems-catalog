How fast do the following dynamics reach R-polarization? There are m user vectors u_j and n creator vectors v_i on the unit sphere in R^d. ## Q338. For every such weakly transitive Γ and every i.i.d. payoff law supported in [0,1…

**Status:** Open · **Kind:** open problem · **Collection** 4

For every such weakly transitive Γ and every i.i.d. payoff law supported in [0,1], does V_n(z) converge almost surely, for all starting vertices z, to a common deterministic constant without any restriction on graph growth?

**Context.** Discovery: Independent dissertation lead already under review before the advisor-lineage search; no genealogy-derived route is claimed. Origin: The author’s explicit generalization of the two Chapter 5 convergence theorems. Chapter 5 incorporates joint doctoral work with Luc Attia, Lyuben Lichev, Dieter Mitsche and Bruno Ziliotto, accepted in February 2025 and published in Dynamic Games and Applications 15 (2025), 1517–1535. This is their newly defined weakly transitive graph class, not a restatement of the old finite-state asymptotic-value theorem. Setup for question 338: Let Γ be a countably infinite acyclic directed graph with uniformly bounded degrees and no sinks. Vertices have i.i.d. payoffs in [0,1], revealed to both players before play. The players alternately move a token along an outgoing edge; Player 2 pays Player 1 the payoff at each newly visited vertex. Let V_n(z) be the value of the n-move average-payoff game from z. Vertices are equivalent when their descendant-induced directed graphs are isomorphic. Weak transitivity means that some vertex z\* and bound M exist such that, from every z, each player can individually force arrival at a vertex equivalent to z\* after an even number of at most M moves, regardless of the opponent.

**Source.** Raimundo Julián Saona Urmeneta. *Robustness of Solutions in Game Theory: Values and Strategies in Partially Observable, Perturbed, Stochastic, and Infinite Games*. Institute of Science and Technology Austria, 2025. Advisor(s): Krishnendu Chatterjee. [primary source](https://research-explorer.ista.ac.at/download/20234/20240/2025_Saona_Raimundo_Thesis.pdf) · [record](https://doi.org/10.15479/AT-ISTA-20234) Location: §5.3, Perspectives, printed p. 107; model and weak transitivity in §5.2, pp. 101–103, Definition 5.2.2.

## Q341. Determine exactly, as a function of F, the smallest integer B(F) such that in ev…

**Status:** Open · **Kind:** open problem · **Collection** 4

Determine exactly, as a function of F, the smallest integer B(F) such that in every such arena, from every vertex from which Player 1 can win with probability one, some almost-surely winning behavioral strategy uses at most B(F) memory states. The dissertation gives an upward-closed-pruned Zielonka-tree upper bound, but leaves its sharpness, or the correct smaller bound with matching witness arenas, unresolved.

**Context.** Discovery path (advisor ascent; student → supervisor):
Raimundo Julián Saona Urmeneta → Krishnendu Chatterjee: https://research-explorer.ista.ac.at/record/20234 Origin: An explicit open question following the author’s improved memory upper bound. The result also appears in his doctoral-period sole-authored FoSSaCS 2007 paper “Optimal Strategy Synthesis in Stochastic Müller Games”; the earlier upward-closed memoryless case is joint work with de Alfaro and Henzinger (2004). This is the sharpness problem for his refinement, not the older pure-strategy Zielonka bound. Setup for question 341: Fix a finite color set C and a family F ⊆ 2^C. A finite turn-based stochastic arena has no dead ends, perfect observation, vertices colored in C, vertices controlled by either player, and chance vertices with fixed successor distributions. Player 1 wins exactly when the colors appearing infinitely often form a set in F. A behavioral M-memory strategy has a fixed initial memory state, deterministic updates from the current vertex and memory, and randomized move choices depending on that vertex and memory; |M| measures memory.
