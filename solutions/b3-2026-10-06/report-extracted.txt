Findings and scope
Three of the twenty exact questions have complete proofs: Q165, Q166 and Q325. The
proofs include stronger statements: strict left-half-plane location of all nonzero path-domination
roots; weighted log-concavity for binary words under arbitrary interval-sum constraints; and equal-
ity cases plus the full attainable root locus for Stanley-nonnegative polynomials.
The remaining work gives several all-parameter partial theorems, complete small-degree classifi-
cations, exact recognition procedures, and finite certificates. The main additional results are total
nonnegativity of order two for both higher-order Stirling triangles for every order parameter; positiv-
ity of both extreme coefficients of every cycle Hankel minor; exact interpolation extrema in degrees
two and three; an infinite Pell family of s-Eulerian representations; and a growing log-concave prefix
for sparse random-graph independence counts with an explicit probability bound.
These are research derivations from the user’s 6 October 2026 handoff. The central proofs were
independently checked within this research run. Source statements and relevant prior results were
checked against primary sources. No assertion of publication priority or exhaustive literature cov-
erage is made. Complete proofs, rather than finite computational agreement, support the three
completed statements.

How to read the certificates
An all-parameter theorem settles its displayed quantifiers.
An exact polynomial root certificate
settles that complete polynomial.
A factorization of a finite matrix into nonnegative bidiagonal
matrices settles every minor of that finite matrix. A passed range of unrelated instances remains
finite evidence for the family.

prove its high-probability limit. The partial random-graph theorem below supplies its own explicit
probability bound. Likewise, the exact diagnostic never turns missing prefix terms into zeros and
never uses leading principal minors alone as a semidefinite-matrix criterion.

Theorem. Let 𝑝(𝑥) have nonnegative integer coefficients and constant term one, and put 𝑁= 𝑝(1).
If 𝑁> 1, then 𝑝= 𝐸𝑠for some positive integer vector of arbitrary length if and only if it equals

1. Choose any ordered factorization 𝑁= 𝑡1 ⋯𝑡𝑞with every 𝑡𝑖≥2.
2. Insert either no entry or one entry equal to one in each of the 𝑞−1 internal gaps.
Here 𝑞≤Ω(𝑁), the number of prime factors counted with multiplicity, and the resulting length
is at most 2Ω(𝑁) −1. If 𝑁= 1, the only possible polynomial is one, represented by any all-ones

Proof. The number of inversion sequences is 𝐸𝑠(1) = ∏𝑖𝑠𝑖. All nonunit entries therefore form

only the stated choices. Conversely every stated choice is a valid positive vector. The length and
factor-count bounds follow directly. □

Boundary-safe exact methods
Real roots. A rational Sturm chain counts distinct roots of the squarefree part. Multiplicities and
roots at zero are retained separately. For nonnegative polynomial coefficients, real nonpositive roots
are exactly the finite PF property.

𝑝(𝑧+ 𝑡). Every root of 𝑝lies in Re 𝑧≤0 exactly when 𝑝(𝑧+ 𝑡) is strictly Hurwitz stable for every

determinant, regarded as a polynomial in 𝑡, having positive first nonzero coefficient. The diagnostic
computes those polynomials exactly. This handles repeated imaginary-axis roots, where a strict test
on 𝑝alone would fail. The default symbolic degree budget is twelve and is explicit in the output.

Most useful remaining proof targets
The next work should focus on a short list of concrete missing statements.
1. Q1158 and Q1159: strengthen total nonnegativity of order two to a uniform factorization,
planar network, or explicit higher-minor formula. Exact Neville factors exist for the comput-
ed blocks, but a symbolic description valid for arbitrary size is missing. The naive ordinary
production matrices have negative entries, so their signs do not directly give the required
certificate.
2. Q1160: interpret the interior coefficients of the cycle Hankel determinants. The two extremes
are already positive by explicit moment arguments; any counterexample would have to occur
strictly between the proved degrees. The related subset negative minor identifies a precise
feature a cycle-specific proof must use.
3. Q164: upgrade the interval-constraint four-functions argument to binomial-normalized ultra-
log-concavity, and address circular intervals for cycles. Ordinary log-concavity alone is insuf-
ficient. The all-length success for Q165 also suggests investigating whether richer interval
recurrences have positive cross-product certificates.
4. Q1441–Q1443: use the compact cube parameterization and product closure to sharpen the
uniform extremal bounds. Fixed-degree compactness gives existence of extrema, but it sup-
plies no bound independent of degree. The explicit lower examples constrain what a proposed
bound can be.
5. Q1262: replace finite volume-factorization recognition by structural criteria for colored multi-
