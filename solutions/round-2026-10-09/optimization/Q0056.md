# Q56: high-precision minimization is NP-hard at rank two

## Result and scope

**Conditional negative resolution.** In the standard binary rational-input model, an additive-error `AMIN` algorithm polynomial in the input length and `log(C_max / epsilon)` for the costs in Q56 would imply **P = NP**. Hardness holds with mode size `n = 2`, CP rank at most two, no sparse term, zero dual weights, positive rational factor entries at most one, and `1/2 <= C_max <= 1`.

The same construction proves the corresponding high-precision hardness of multimarginal optimal transport even when all marginals are uniform on two points. This is a complexity implication, not an unconditional proof that P differs from NP.

Evidence: a written reduction, a separately reviewed gap calculation, and exact rational checks. No external peer review is claimed. Date: 2026-10-09.

## Source alignment

The catalog asks whether the error dependence for a fixed-rank-plus-sparse approximate minimum oracle can improve from polynomial in `C_max / epsilon` to polynomial in its logarithm. The authoritative thesis is Jason M. Altschuler, *Transport and Beyond: Efficient Optimization over Probability Distributions* (MIT, 2022), §6.7.2, p. 190, discussion following Theorem 6.7.4. The published version with Enric Boix-Adsera states the same question in §7.2 after Theorem 7.4:

- [Thesis, author-hosted PDF](https://jasonaltschuler.github.io/altschuler_PhD_thesis.pdf).
- [Published paper: Polynomial-time algorithms for multimarginal optimal transport problems with structure](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-022-01868-7), Definition 7.2 and the discussion following Theorem 7.4.

The definition permits a supplied sum of a fixed number of rank-one tensors. The size bounds on the low-rank and sparse components prevent cancellation of huge entries. The construction below uses positive terms and no sparse component, so it satisfies those bounds directly. `AMIN` returns an approximation to the optimal **value**; the proof uses only a two-sided additive estimate. An oracle returning a feasible approximately minimizing index also suffices.

No existing result on Q56 was found in this repository at base commit `8967f4f70776856b3dfc0b2494ed73167b15ee0c`. The source's established hardness for *variable* rank does not by itself prove this fixed-rank statement.

## The reduction

An instance of **PRODUCT PARTITION** consists of positive integers `a_1,...,a_k`. It asks whether some subset has the same product as its complement. Put

$$
P=\prod_{i=1}^k a_i.
$$

If `P` is not a square, the instance is immediately a NO instance. Otherwise compute the integer `t` such that `P = t^2`.

Index each mode by a bit `b_i in {0,1}` and define

$$
C_b=\frac12\left(\prod_{i=1}^k a_i^{-b_i}
                 +\prod_{i=1}^k a_i^{-(1-b_i)}\right).
\tag{1}
$$

This is the explicit sum of two rank-one tensors. For example, their mode vectors are `(1,1/a_i)` and `(1/a_i,1)`, with the factor `1/2` absorbed into the first mode of each term. Set `R=C`, `S=0`, and all supplied dual weights to zero.

For an index `b`, let

$$
A_b=\prod_{i:b_i=1}a_i,\qquad B_b=P/A_b.
$$

Both are positive integers, and (1) is exactly

$$
C_b=\frac{A_b+B_b}{2P}.
\tag{2}
$$

### YES and NO gap

Since `A_b B_b = t^2`, the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives

$$
A_b+B_b\ge 2t,
$$

with equality if and only if `A_b=B_b=t`. Thus a YES instance has

$$
\min_b C_b=\frac{1}{t}.
\tag{3}
$$

For a NO instance, `A_b+B_b` is an integer strictly greater than `2t`. Therefore every index satisfies

$$
C_b\ge\frac{1}{t}+\frac{1}{2P}.
\tag{4}
$$

Query the proposed oracle at

$$
\varepsilon=\frac1{8P}.
$$

If `v` is any answer with `|v-min_b C_b| <= epsilon`, then

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{YES:}&v\le 1/t+1/(8P),\\
\text{NO:}&v\ge 1/t+3/(8P).
\end{array}
$$

Comparing `v` with `1/t+1/(4P)` decides the instance, with a nonzero separation on both sides. If the oracle instead returns an epsilon-optimal index, evaluate its rational cost and apply the same threshold.

### Size and numerical promises

For every subset, `1 <= A_b <= P`. The identity

$$
(P+1)-(A_b+P/A_b)=\frac{(A_b-1)(P-A_b)}{A_b}\ge0
$$

shows that the maximal entry, attained by an empty or full subset, is

$$
C_{\max}=\frac{P+1}{2P}\in[1/2,1].
$$

All factor entries are positive rationals at most one. There are `4k` factor coordinates and their total binary encoding length is polynomial in the input length `L = sum_i ceil(log_2(a_i+1))`. Computing `P`, its integer square root, the threshold, and epsilon also takes polynomial time in `L`. Finally,

$$
\frac{C_{\max}}{\varepsilon}=4(P+1),\qquad
\log(C_{\max}/\varepsilon)=O(L).
$$

Consequently a polynomial-time high-precision oracle would decide PRODUCT PARTITION in polynomial time. This proves the claimed implication.

## Why the source problem is NP-hard

PRODUCT PARTITION is proved NP-complete in C. T. Ng, M. S. Barketau, T. C. E. Cheng and M. Y. Kovalyov, [*Product Partition and related problems of scheduling and systems reliability: Computational complexity and approximation*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2010.05.034), European Journal of Operational Research 207 (2010), 601-604. Only ordinary NP-hardness is needed here.

For completeness, a direct reduction from the standard NP-complete EXACT COVER BY 3-SETS problem gives that ordinary hardness. Assign a distinct prime to each universe element. Encode each available triple by the product `a_i` of its three primes, put `B` equal to the product of all universe primes, and put `P_0=prod_i a_i`. A subset of the triples is an exact cover exactly when its encoded product is `B`.

Append the two integers `P_0` and `B^2` to the list of triple products. The full product of this new list is `(P_0 B)^2`. A subset with product `P_0 B` cannot contain neither appended integer, because the product of any subset of the original list is at most `P_0`. It cannot contain both, because their product already exceeds `P_0 B`. If it contains only `P_0`, its original triple products multiply to `B`. If it contains only `B^2`, the complementary original triple products multiply to `B`. This establishes equivalence. Empty or immediately infeasible instances may be handled separately. The first polynomially many primes and every displayed product have polynomial binary encoding length.

## Corollary: uniform-marginal optimal transport

The cost is invariant under bit complementation: `C_b=C_(1-b)`. Let `b*` attain its minimum. The coupling that places probability `1/2` on `b*` and `1/2` on `1-b*` has every one-dimensional marginal uniform on `{0,1}` and has expected cost `C_b*`. Every coupling has expected cost at least the smallest tensor entry. Hence

$$
\min_{\gamma:\,\gamma_i=\operatorname{Unif}\{0,1\}}
\mathbb E_\gamma C_b=\min_b C_b.
$$

The same YES/NO gap and encoding argument therefore apply directly to this fixed-marginal MOT problem. No separate optimization-oracle equivalence theorem is needed for this corollary.

## Exact example

The YES list `(2,3,6)` and NO list `(2,2,9)` both have `P=36` and `t=6`. Their minima are respectively `1/6` and `13/72`. The precision `1/288` and threshold `25/144` separate even two-sided approximate values.

Run `python solutions/round-2026-10-09/optimization/check_q0056.py`. The result rules out the proposed general high-precision guarantee under P != NP. It does not rule out the source's polynomial-in-`1/epsilon` approximation algorithm, faster algorithms under additional input restrictions, or the hypothetical equality P = NP. A bounded-error randomized high-precision oracle would instead imply NP is contained in BPP; it does not imply P = NP by this argument.
