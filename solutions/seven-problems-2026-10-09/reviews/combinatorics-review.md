# Independent internal review: Q256 and Q257

Date: 2026-10-09. Reviewer: a separate AI research agent from the author of the combinatorics reports. This is internal mathematical review, not external peer review, formal verification, or a novelty certification.

**Verdict:** accept Q256 as a proposed complete finite-state characterization and Q257 as partial theorems. The arbitrary-order claims follow from the written arguments. Finite subset enumeration supplies independent controls for the algorithm and constructions.

## Source and scope

The reviewer independently checked the open-neighborhood definition in the [authors' conference abstract](https://www.math.fau.edu/combinatorics/abstracts/osborn-57.pdf) and in [Chartrand–Salehi–Zhang, *Realizability in Proper Total Domination in Graphs*](https://www.dmlett.com/archive/v17/DML26_v17_pp37-44.pdf), pages 1–2. The abstract describes the earlier caterpillar criterion as sufficient. Publisher-indexed results identify Questions 1 and 3 of [Osborn–Zhang, *Proper Total Domination in Trees*](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-8994/17/9/1429); direct publisher retrieval was rate-limited, so no new full-PDF inspection of that article or the thesis is claimed in this review.

The later realizability paper concerns unrestricted connected graphs and also the maximum proper-total parameter. It does not justify transferring its attainable pairs to trees. The reports correctly distinguish this scope, acknowledge that the small examples overlap published families, and retain the catalog's authoritative thesis references.

## Q256: local conditions, finite cap, and automaton

- Every leaf has neighbor sum equal to the membership bit of its support. Thus a support must be selected and must have its own neighbor sum at least two. A spine vertex without leaves has neighbor sum equal to the sum of its two neighboring membership bits. These are exactly the local conditions used; vertices inside the selected set are not exempted from total domination.
- For a support with at least one selected spine neighbor, an excessive selected-leaf count or neighbor sum can be reduced by choosing one of the three sums $2,3,4$. At most two spine neighbors prohibit values, all candidate leaf counts are nonnegative, and the new count is strictly smaller. For a support with no selected spine neighbor, its neighboring spine vertices cannot themselves support leaves and have sums at most two; reducing the count to three is valid. Changing pendant-leaf membership changes only the support's neighbor sum.
- These arguments apply to a minimum witness and establish both bounds simultaneously: selected leaf count at most three and spine neighbor sum at most four. Transferring witnesses between the original and truncated tree preserves feasibility and the minimum. Keeping at least one leaf at every original support is essential and is respected.
- The claimed sharpness examples check directly. The word $(3,0,0,1)$ forces membership $(1,0,1,1)$ and sums $(3,2,1,2)$; truncating the first count to two is infeasible. The word $(1,1,2,1)$ forces sums $(2,3,4,2)$ and selects all nine vertices.
- The state after position $i$ is $(x_i,x_{i+1},q_i)$. A transition overlaps precisely the required membership bit, checks the current local count, and prohibits equal sums on the new spine edge. Initial and terminal conditions impose the two absent endpoint neighbors. This handles a one-vertex spine as well.
- The initialization cost $a+q-b$ and transition cost $b+r-a-d$ each count exactly one spine membership and that position's selected leaves. They count each selected vertex once. The state set and per-state transitions have fixed size, so the stated linear arithmetic-operation bound and linear witness-storage bound are justified.

The implementation was read against this recurrence, including predecessor reconstruction, infeasible states, original vertex labels with large multiplicities, and input validity. The finite checker computes open-neighborhood counts directly from subsets; it does not use the recurrence as its expected answer.

## Q257: exclusions and finite representatives

1. **Strict separation.** The induced forest on a proper total dominating set has no isolated vertices and hence has a leaf $u$ with neighbor sum one. Every graph neighbor of $u$ has neighbor sum at least two by properness. Removing $u$ leaves a total dominating set. This proves $\gamma_t+1\le\gamma_{pt}$ without assuming connectedness of the selected forest.
2. **Connected total dominating sets.** An outside vertex must have a neighbor in the connected induced subtree. Two such neighbors, or an edge between two outside vertices, would create a cycle. The stated leaf-attachment lemma follows.
3. **The $a=2$ slice.** A dominating edge limits the tree to stars and double stars. A star requires exactly two selected leaves; a genuine double star requires unequal positive selected-leaf counts on its two supports. The only finite proper minima are three and five, with $K_2$ and $P_4$ excluded.
4. **The $a=3$ slice.** An isolate-free induced forest on three vertices is $P_3$. Both endpoint leaf counts must be positive, or the total parameter drops to two. With a middle leaf, three selected leaves give minimum six. Without a middle leaf, both outer sums must differ from one and two; selecting the middle spine vertex and two leaves on each side gives minimum seven, and removing that middle vertex cannot improve feasibility or cost. The asserted cases and nonexistence boundaries are exact at arbitrary order.
5. **The core bound.** A minimum total dominating set of size $a$ has $c\le a/2$ induced components. After removing leaves outside the set and contracting these components, every remaining outside core vertex retains degree at least two and has a neighbor in a contracted component. With $r$ such vertices and edge counts $m,e$, the equations $m+e=c+r-1$, $m\ge r$, $m+2e\ge2r$ yield $r\le2c-2$. This proves the core bound $2a-2$, even when some selected vertices are original leaves.
6. **Leaf reduction.** At a support of core degree $d$, there are $d+1$ candidate proper neighbor sums and at most $d$ core-neighbor exclusions. A minimum proper set consequently needs at most $d+2$ selected leaves there. A minimum ordinary total dominating set needs at most one. Retaining up to $d+2$ actual leaves preserves both parameters in both directions, including infeasibility, and does not change the core or its support set.
7. **Order bound.** If the core has $h$ vertices and $p$ supports, its non-support vertices have degree at least two. Thus the sum of support core degrees is at most $2p-2$, also for the one-vertex core. With $h\le2a-2$ and $p\le a$, the reduced order is at most $h+4p-2\le6a-4$. This is a finite witness bound, not a sharp bound or a closed classification of pairs.

No unresolved proof defect was found. The catalog should not promote the observed pairs for $a\ge4$ to an exhaustive classification. In particular, the finite order-13 checks do not reach the representative bound for all relevant values of $a$, and Q257 remains partial.
