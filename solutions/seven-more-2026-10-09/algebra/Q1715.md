# Q1715: a 13-dimensional counterexample to the property-F characterization

Date: 2026-10-09. Scope: the full proposed equivalence is false over every field
of characteristic zero. The counterexample satisfies property F for **every**
generating pair, including pairs with higher-degree terms.

## 1. Source and claim

The source is Burde, Dekimpe and Moens, *Commutative post-Lie algebra structures
and linear equations for nilpotent Lie algebras*, Definition 5.1 and the final
open problem in Section 5. In the [arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/html/1711.01964v1),
property F requires every solution in the derived algebra of

\[
 [X,U]+[Y,V]=0,\qquad [X,V]+[Y,W]=0
 \tag{1}
\]

to be central, for every generating pair \(X,Y\). The conjectural converse says
that this forces the algebra to be free nilpotent on two generators.

**Theorem.** There is a 13-dimensional, two-generated, nilpotent Lie algebra of
class five satisfying property F. Its center has dimension five. It is not
isomorphic to any free nilpotent Lie algebra on two generators.

The proof below uses explicit structure constants, elementary linear algebra,
and the grading. It does not infer the universal assertion about generating
pairs from a finite search.

## 2. Definition of the algebra

Let \(k\) be any field of characteristic zero. Define a vector space with the
following ordered homogeneous basis:

| Degree | Basis |
|---|---|
| 1 | \(x,y\) |
| 2 | \(z\) |
| 3 | \(u,v\) |
| 4 | \(a,b,c\) |
| 5 | \(p,q,r,s,w\) |

Define an alternating bilinear bracket by the following nonzero brackets and
their skew counterparts. Every unlisted bracket between basis elements is zero.

\[
\begin{aligned}
[x,y]&=z, & [x,z]&=u, & [y,z]&=v,\\
[x,u]&=a, & [x,v]&=b, & [y,u]&=b, & [y,v]&=c,\\
[x,a]&=p, & [x,b]&=q, & [x,c]&=r+w,\\
[y,a]&=q, & [y,b]&=r, & [y,c]&=s, & [z,v]&=w.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

In particular, \([z,u]=0\). Denote this algebra by \(L\), and its degree-\(d\)
subspace by \(L_d\).

### Jacobi identity

All brackets preserve degree, and degrees above five vanish. Thus a Jacobi
identity with total degree greater than five is automatic. A Jacobi expression
with two equal entries is zero by skew-symmetry, so multilinearity leaves only
the three types with distinct basis elements and total degree at most five:

\[
\begin{aligned}
J(x,y,z)&=[x,v]-[y,u]=b-b=0,\\
J(x,y,u)&=[x,b]-[y,a]+[u,z]=q-q=0,\\
J(x,y,v)&=[x,c]-[y,b]+[v,z]=(r+w)-r-w=0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore (2) defines a Lie algebra.

Every basis vector is an iterated bracket of \(x,y\); for \(w\), use
\(w=[z,v]\). Consequently

\[
[L,L]=\bigoplus_{d=2}^{5}L_d,\qquad \dim L/[L,L]=2.
\]

The grading gives class at most five, and
\([x,[x,[x,[x,y]]]]=p\ne0\) gives class exactly five.

## 3. The three injective graded maps

For \(d=2,3,4\), let

\[
T_d:L_d^3\longrightarrow L_{d+1}^2,\qquad
(U,V,W)\longmapsto([x,U]+[y,V],\,[x,V]+[y,W]).
\]

We show that every \(T_d\) is injective.

For \(d=2\), put \((U,V,W)=(Az,Bz,Cz)\). Then

\[
T_2(U,V,W)=(Au+Bv,\,Bu+Cv).
\]

Independence of \(u,v\) forces \(A=B=C=0\).

For \(d=3\), write

\[
U=Au+Bv,\quad V=Cu+Dv,\quad W=Eu+Fv.
\]

The two output vectors are

\[
Aa+(B+C)b+Dc,\qquad Ca+(D+E)b+Fc.
\]

Their coefficients successively give
\(A=D=C=F=B=E=0\).

For \(d=4\), write the coefficient rows of \(U,V,W\) in the basis
\((a,b,c)\) as \((A,B,C),(D,E,F),(G,H,I)\), respectively. The outputs are

\[
\begin{aligned}
&Ap+(B+D)q+(C+E)r+Fs+Cw,\\
&Dp+(E+G)q+(F+H)r+Is+Fw.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

The first row gives \(A=F=C=E=0\) and \(B=-D\). The second then gives
\(D=G=H=I=0\), and hence \(B=0\). Thus \(T_4\) is injective as well.

These three injectivity statements also imply \(Z(L)=L_5\). A central element
with nonzero leading component in degree one would have that component killed
by both \(\operatorname{ad}x\) and \(\operatorname{ad}y\), which (2) forbids.
A nonzero leading component \(A\in L_d\), for \(2\le d\le4\), would similarly
give \(T_d(A,0,0)=0\), contradicting injectivity. Every element of \(L_5\) is
central by degree.

## 4. Every linear change of generators

The following elementary observation is what permits a universal statement.
Suppose \(x',y'\) are any basis of \(L_1\), written as

\[
(x',y')=(x,y)P,\qquad P\in\operatorname{GL}_2(k).
\]

Encode a triple in a symmetric matrix with vector entries:

\[
S=\begin{pmatrix}U&V\\V&W\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For this notation, the product of a row of generators and a matrix of vectors
means taking the indicated linear combinations of brackets. If the two
equations for \((x',y')\) vanish, then

\[
(x,y)PS=0,\qquad (x,y)(PSP^{\mathsf T})=0.
\]

The matrix \(PSP^{\mathsf T}\) is symmetric. Injectivity of \(T_d\) for
\((x,y)\) therefore makes all its entries zero. Since \(P\) is invertible,
\(S=0\). Thus the corresponding degree-\(d\) map is injective for every basis
\((x',y')\) of \(L_1\).

This does not assert that every such linear change extends to an automorphism
of \(L\); that assertion is unnecessary.

## 5. Every generating pair, including higher-degree perturbations

Let \(X,Y\) be an arbitrary generating pair of \(L\). Their degree-one parts
\(x',y'\) form a basis of \(L_1\), because \(L/[L,L]\cong L_1\).

Take a solution \((U,V,W)\in[L,L]^3\) of (1). Suppose one of its components
has a nonzero term below degree five, and let \(d\in\{2,3,4\}\) be the smallest
degree occurring in any component. The degree-\(d+1\) parts of (1) are exactly

\[
[x',U_d]+[y',V_d]=0,\qquad [x',V_d]+[y',W_d]=0.
\]

Indeed, every higher-degree part of \(X,Y\) brackets with these vectors in
degree at least \(d+2\); lower-degree parts of \(U,V,W\) are zero by choice of
\(d\). The preceding section forces
\(U_d=V_d=W_d=0\), a contradiction. Hence

\[
U,V,W\in L_5=Z(L).
\]

This proves property F for every generating pair.

## 6. Why the algebra is not free nilpotent

Any free nilpotent algebra isomorphic to \(L\) would have two generators and
class five. The homogeneous dimensions of the free Lie algebra on two
generators through degree five are \(2,1,2,3,6\), so
\(\dim F_{2,5}=14\). These dimensions follow directly from Witt's formula

\[
\dim (F_2)_d=\frac1d\sum_{e\mid d}\mu(e)2^{d/e}.
\]

They are also recorded in the source, immediately before Section 5. In
contrast, \(\dim L=13\). Thus \(L\) is not any \(F_{2,c}\), disproving the
proposed equivalence.

For perspective, \(L\) can be obtained from \(F_{2,5}\) by killing the central
element \([[x,y],[x,[x,y]]]\). The table definition and proof above do not
depend on identifying this quotient.
