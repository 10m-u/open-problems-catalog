# Q2305: partial inner automorphisms of matrix monoids

Date: 2026-10-09. Scope: every field \(F\) and every finite-dimensional
\(F\)-vector space \(V\). This gives an explicit structural model, its equality
relation, and its multiplication, for \(\operatorname{Inn}(\operatorname{End}_F(V))\).

## 1. Source convention

Araújo, Bentz, Kinyon, Konieczny, Malheiro and Mercier define
\(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\) to be generated by the maps

\[
\phi_{g,h}:D_{g,h}\longrightarrow D_{h,g},\quad a\longmapsto hag,
\qquad D_{g,h}=\{a:gha=a=agh\}.
\tag{1}
\]

See [Definition 3.8 and Problem 7.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00041v1).
For a matrix monoid every generator contains the zero matrix in its domain and
fixes it. Consequently the zero of the inverse monoid is the identity on
\(\{0\}\), **not the empty map**. We use ordinary function composition,
with the rightmost map applied first, throughout.

## 2. The model

Write \(S=\operatorname{End}_F(V)\), \(n=\dim V\), and for subspaces
\(U,W\le V\) put

\[
D(U,W)=\{a\in S:\operatorname{im}a\subseteq U,\ W\subseteq\ker a\}
       \cong \operatorname{Hom}_F(V/W,U).
\]

These are subsemigroups as well as vector subspaces. Define the semilattice

\[
\mathcal E(V)=\{S,\{0\}\}\ \cup\
\{D(U,W):0<U<V,\ 0<W<V\}.
\tag{2}
\]

Repeated entries are counted once; when \(n=0\), the two exceptional domains
coincide. For \(H\in\operatorname{GL}(V)\), let
\(c_H(a)=HaH^{-1}\).

**Theorem.** The following equality holds as inverse monoids of partial maps:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Inn}(S)
=\{\left.c_H\right|_D:H\in\operatorname{GL}(V),\ D\in\mathcal E(V)\}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

This is an explicit isomorphism description, rather than just a generating
set: equality of representatives is given below by linear equations, and
multiplication uses intersections, sums, and invertible matrices.

For a nonzero domain \(D(U,W)\), including \(S=D(V,0)\), one has

\[
\left.c_H\right|_{D(U,W)}=\left.c_K\right|_{D(U,W)}
\iff
K^{-1}H=\lambda I+N
\tag{4}
\]

for some \(\lambda\in F^\times\) and \(N\in\operatorname{End}(V)\) satisfying
\(N(U)=0\) and \(\operatorname{im}N\subseteq W\). The invertibility of
\(\lambda I+N\) is already ensured by \(K^{-1}H\).
All representatives on \(\{0\}\) coincide. Distinct nonzero domains give
distinct partial maps.

For \(D=D(U,W)\) and \(E=D(U',W')\), multiplication is

\[
\left.c_H\right|_D\circ\left.c_K\right|_E
=\left.c_{HK}\right|_{D(U'\cap K^{-1}U,\ W'+K^{-1}W)}.
\tag{5}
\]

If the resulting rectangular domain is zero, it is replaced by the unique
zero element. The full domain is represented by \((V,0)\), so (5) covers it
as well. Inversion is

\[
\left(\left.c_H\right|_{D(U,W)}\right)^{-1}
=\left.c_{H^{-1}}\right|_{D(HU,HW)}.
\tag{6}
\]

Thus (2), (4), and (5) describe the desired inverse monoid entirely in terms
of subspaces and linear maps on \(V\). In particular, it is factorisable:
every element is a restriction of a unit. For \(n\ge1\), its unit group is
\(\operatorname{PGL}(V)\).

## 3. Domains of the original generators

For \(t=gh\), the two equations \(ta=a=at\) say exactly

\[
\operatorname{im}a\subseteq\ker(t-I),\qquad
\operatorname{im}(t-I)\subseteq\ker a.
\]

Hence

\[
D_{g,h}=D(\ker(t-I),\operatorname{im}(t-I)).
\tag{7}
\]

The two displayed subspaces have complementary **dimensions** by rank-nullity;
they need not have zero intersection. This distinction includes matrices with
nontrivial Jordan blocks at eigenvalue one.

Conversely, let \(E,K\le V\) satisfy \(\dim E+\dim K=n\). Choose a linear
map \(L:V\to V\) with kernel \(E\) and image \(K\): any isomorphism
\(V/E\cong K\) provides one. Put \(t=I+L\). Then

\[
D_{t,I}=D(E,K),\qquad \phi_{t,I}(a)=at=a.
\tag{8}
\]

Therefore the identity on each such rectangular domain is already an original
generator. This includes the full domain and the zero domain.

## 4. Every original generator extends to a unit

Fix \(g,h\in S\), and put \(t=gh\), \(s=hg\). Choose an integer \(m\ge n\).
Finite-dimensional linear algebra gives the Fitting decompositions

\[
V=T\oplus N=\operatorname{im}t^m\oplus\ker t^m,
\quad
V=T'\oplus N'=\operatorname{im}s^m\oplus\ker s^m.
\]

The maps \(t|_T\) and \(s|_{T'}\) are invertible, while their restrictions
to \(N,N'\) are nilpotent. The relations

\[
ht=sh,\qquad tg=gs
\]

show that \(h(T)\subseteq T'\) and \(g(N')\subseteq N\).
The restriction \(h|_T\) is injective: \(hv=0\) implies \(tv=ghv=0\),
and \(t|_T\) is invertible. It is also surjective onto \(T'\), because

\[
T'=\operatorname{im}s^{m+1}
   =h\bigl(\operatorname{im}(t^m g)\bigr)\subseteq h(T).
\]

It follows that \(\dim N=\dim N'\). Extend \(h|_T\) to an invertible map
\(H:V\to V\) by choosing any isomorphism \(N\to N'\).

For \(a\in D_{g,h}\), the relations \(a=ta=at\) imply

\[
\operatorname{im}a\subseteq T,\qquad a(N)=0,\qquad at=a.
\]

If \(v\in T\), then on the vector \(hv\in T'\),

\[
HaH^{-1}(hv)=hav=h(at)v=hag(hv).
\]

On \(N'\), both operators vanish: the first because \(H^{-1}N'=N\),
the second because \(gN'\subseteq N\). Hence

\[
\phi_{g,h}=\left.c_H\right|_{D_{g,h}}.
\tag{9}
\]

The argument uses neither algebraic closure nor eigenvalue diagonalization.

## 5. The generated domain semilattice is exactly (2)

Rectangular domains satisfy

\[
D(U,W)\cap D(U',W')=D(U\cap U',W+W'),
\tag{10}
\]

and \(c_H(D(U,W))=D(HU,HW)\). These operations preserve (2): a nonzero
intersection involving a proper domain still has a proper image subspace and
a nonzero kernel subspace. Therefore (9) and composition show that every
element of \(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\) belongs to the right side of (3).

For the other inclusion, it remains to realize the identity on every domain
in (2). Let \(0<U<V\), \(0<W<V\), with dimensions \(u,w\).

If \(u+w\le n\), choose finitely many subspaces \(E_i\supseteq U\), each
of dimension \(n-w\), whose intersection is \(U\). Such a family exists:
in a complement of \(U\), take the spans of all coordinate subsets of size
\(n-w-u\); their intersection is zero since \(w>0\). Each \(D(E_i,W)\)
is an original identity domain by (8), and (10) gives

\[
D(U,W)=\bigcap_i D(E_i,W).
\]

If \(u+w\ge n\), choose finitely many subspaces \(K_i\subseteq W\), each
of dimension \(n-u\), whose sum is \(W\). Spans of all coordinate subsets
of size \(n-u\) suffice; this size is positive because \(U<V\).
Again (8) and (10) give

\[
D(U,W)=\bigcap_i D(U,K_i).
\]

The equality case allows one domain in either construction. Identities on
these finite intersections are compositions of original identity generators.
Finally, every full conjugation \(c_H\) is the original generator
\(\phi_{H^{-1},H}\). Composing it with an identity on \(D\) gives every map
on the right side of (3). This proves the theorem.

## 6. Proof of the equality relation

A nonzero rectangular space determines its defining pair uniquely: the span
of its images is \(U\), and the intersection of its kernels is \(W\).
This follows from its rank-one maps \(a=u\otimes\ell\), with \(u\in U\)
and \(\ell\in W^\perp\subseteq V^*\).

For equal domains, put \(C=K^{-1}H\). Equality of the two partial maps is
equivalent to \(C\) commuting with every \(u\otimes\ell\):

\[
(Cu)\otimes\ell=u\otimes(\ell C).
\tag{11}
\]

Fixing one nonzero \(\ell\) in (11) shows that \(C\) acts as a single scalar
\(\lambda\) on \(U\). Fixing one nonzero \(u\) then shows that
\(\ell C=\lambda\ell\) for every \(\ell\in W^\perp\). Invertibility of
\(C\) forces \(\lambda\ne0\). Thus \(N=C-\lambda I\) kills \(U\),
and its image lies in \(W\). Conversely these two conditions give
\(Na=aN=0\) for every \(a\in D(U,W)\), proving (11). This establishes (4).

Formula (5) follows by requiring \(a\in E\) and \(KaK^{-1}\in D\), and
then applying (10). Formula (6) follows by reversing the conjugation.

## 7. Small dimensions and finite-field cardinalities

For \(n=0\), \(S\) is a singleton and its partial inner automorphism monoid
is trivial. For \(n=1\), every conjugation is the identity and there are just
two elements: the identity on \(F\) and the identity on \(\{0\}\).

For \(n=2\) and \(F=\mathbb F_q\), every proper nonzero domain is a pair
of lines \((U,W)\). There are \((q+1)^2\) such domains. If \(U=W\), its
pointwise stabilizer in \(\operatorname{GL}_2(q)\), as given by (4), has order
\(q(q-1)\); hence there are \(q^2-1\) maps with that domain. If \(U\ne W\),
the stabilizer has order \((q-1)^2\), hence \(q(q+1)\) maps. Adding the
\(q(q^2-1)\) units and the zero gives

\[
|\operatorname{Inn}(M_2(\mathbb F_q))|
=1+q(q^2-1)+(q+1)(q^2-1)+q(q+1)q(q+1)
=q^4+4q^3+2q^2-2q.
\]

finitely generated semigroups.
