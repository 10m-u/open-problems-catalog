# Q3678: oriented-matroid circuit-polytope log-concavity

The frozen statement cb6335b6f3fa2c658fa4 is the full Conjecture 3.11
of [Escobar--McWhirter, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00108v2): all finite
oriented matroids with a nonempty signed-circuit set, in every dimension.
The polytope is the convex hull of **sign** vectors in {0,+1,-1}^E;
the absolute coefficients of a represented dependence are discarded.
Its lattice is span_R(P) intersected with Z^E, as required by the exact
catalog statement. A proper sublattice generated by vertices is not
silently substituted. Loops and direct sums are permitted, and zero
coordinates of coloops contribute no extra dimension.

The source already proves the type-A cocircuit/complete-graph family
has Eulerian h*, and checks all catalog oriented matroids on at most
seven elements. Replaying those controls counts zero. The earlier B3
initial and continuation packets have no Q3678 research route to recount.
The new attempts below are sequential; partials do not reset the count.

## A1: reduce each connected circuit polytope to a lattice zonotope

The source's solved complete-graph cocircuit family is integrally
equivalent to a graphic zonotope. A proposed full-target route is to
split off direct-sum components, then show each connected circuit
polytope is a lattice zonotope. The [Beck--Jochemko--McCullough theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.08596)
would then give real-rooted h* and hence the required coefficient
inequalities for the connected pieces. This proposed geometric reduction
fails already on a connected, representable, regular oriented matroid;
it is not merely an obstruction from a disconnected crosspolytope.

For N>=2 take the rank-one matrix (1,1,...,1). Its minimal dependencies
have precisely two nonzero coordinates of opposite signs, so its circuit
polytope is

    R_N=conv{e_i-e_j: i!=j}.

Every two elements are joined by a circuit, so the matroid is connected.
Its dimension is d=N-1; its span is sum(z_i)=0 and the ambient saturated
lattice is the root lattice {z in Z^N: sum(z_i)=0}. The projection deleting
the last coordinate is a lattice isomorphism to Z^d. It also shows that
the circuit roots generate this entire lattice.

The exposed face z_1=1 consists exactly of e_1-e_j, j=2,...,N, and is
an (N-2)-simplex. At N=4 it is a triangle. Every exposed face of a
Minkowski sum of segments is itself a sum of segments: maximize the
functional on each segment separately. In particular every two-face
of a zonotope is centrally symmetric. A triangle is not. Therefore
R_4 is not affinely equivalent to any zonotope, and no integral change
of coordinates can repair this connected counterexample. For N>4 the
simplex facet contains triangular two-faces, with the same obstruction.

The target inequality nevertheless holds on this **entire** family.
The following calculation supplies its exact Ehrhart numerator rather
than extrapolating from the N=4 witness.

### All-size root-lattice calculation

The convex hull description is equivalent to

    R_N={z: sum z_i=0, sum_i max(z_i,0)<=1}.

One containment is immediate on vertices and convexity. For the other,
write any nonzero such vector as a nonnegative combination of roots by
matching positive coordinates to negative coordinates; the total weight
is its positive sum, at most one. The unused weight multiplies zero,
which lies in the convex hull by central symmetry.

Let f_N(a) count integer vectors with zero sum and positive sum a.
Constant-term extraction in an auxiliary x gives

    sum_(a>=0) f_N(a)t^a
      =CT_x [sum_(z in Z) x^z t^max(z,0)]^N
      =(1-t)^N sum_(j>=0) binom(N+j-1,N-1)^2 t^j.

Here the single-coordinate series is
(1-t)/[(1-tx)(1-x^(-1))]. Taking its constant term pairs equal total
positive and negative exponents in the two expansions. Each fixed t
coefficient has a finite contributing constant term, so no convergence
or exchange of divergent numeric series is being used. Passing from
shells to balls adds a factor 1/(1-t).

For d=N-1, the elementary identity

    sum_(j>=0) binom(j+d,d)^2 t^j
       =[sum_(k=0)^d binom(d,k)^2 t^k]/(1-t)^(2d+1)

can be proved by differentiating t^d/(1-t)^(d+1) exactly d times and
dividing by d!. The product rule gives numerator
sum_j binom(d,j)binom(d+j,j)t^j(1-t)^(d-j). Its t^k coefficient is
binom(d,k) times the kth forward difference of binom(d+j,d) at j=0.
Repeated Pascal identities make that difference binom(d,k), proving
the identity. Thus

    h*_(R_N)(t)=sum_(k=0)^(N-1) binom(N-1,k)^2 t^k.

At every internal index, the successive ratios binom(d,k+1)/binom(d,k)
decrease. Their squares do also, proving strict log-concavity for d>=2.
For d=1 there is no internal inequality. This is a known type-A root
family with a self-contained derivation, not a novel closure claim.

### Full-target outcome

The connected-zonotope sufficient reduction is false. The all-size
binomial-square calculation does not extend to arbitrary oriented
matroids, nor does real representability or regularity force a zonotope.
A different positive mechanism is needed for the remaining polytopes.
This is one failed full-target geometric route, with one exact family
retained; its individual dimensions and computational checks are not
separate attempts. [check_circuit_a1.py](check_circuit_a1.py) records
the actual circuit axioms, root-lattice counts and candidate obstruction.

Seek an actual failure in the first dense cographic families with more
elements, counting the complete Ehrhart numerator rather than a prefix.
Take every connected five-vertex graph with eight, nine or ten edges.
There are 56 labelled graphs and exactly four isomorphism types:
K_5, K_5 minus one edge, and K_5 minus two adjacent or two disjoint edges.
The complete-graph case is the source's known calibration and contributes
zero new attempts; the three noncomplete types are fresh full-row
constructions outside the stated at-most-seven-element catalog range.

For a graph G, a bond is a cut whose two induced sides are connected.
These are the signed circuits of its dual graphical oriented matroid.
Fix vertex 5 at potential zero. A bond S has anchored potential vector
1_S(i)-1_S(5), i=1,...,4; the gradient on each oriented edge is its
signed circuit vector. Connectedness makes this map injective on the
real span. Moreover its saturated integer gradient lattice is exactly
the image of Z^4: integer gradients give integer potentials by summing
edge differences along a path from the fixed vertex. Thus integer
potential counting uses the exact catalog lattice, not a replacement
vertex-generated sublattice.

## A3: a universal reflexive reciprocal-gamma certificate

The source's solved complete-graph family is reflexive with an Eulerian
numerator. Try extending that setting to every circuit polytope: obtain
reflexivity and then a reciprocal gamma-polynomial root/interlacing
certificate, which would imply real-rooted h* and the requested
log-concavity. Gamma positivity alone would only imply unimodality;
the proposed full certificate requires the additional root control.
The necessary preliminary reflexivity and reciprocity fail on actual
connected regular examples in an unbounded family.

For N>=4 let G_N=K_N minus edge ab; choose its last vertex as the anchored
potential zero, distinct from a,b. In the cographic lattice coordinates
of A2, its vertices are all positive/negative zero-one vectors except
w=e_a+e_b and -w. Indeed every proper induced side of K_N is connected
except the two-element side {a,b}; deleting this cut eliminates exactly
that opposite vertex pair. Its span and saturated lattice are R^(N-1)
and Z^(N-1), respectively. All singleton cuts remain, providing the
coordinate vectors and full dimension.

Consider the primitive normal and right side

    eta=(N-3, 1, -1,...,-1),   b=N-3,

where a,b are the first two coordinates and the remaining N-3 are
unanchored vertices. For a positive zero-one vertex, if a,b are both
present then at least one negative-normal coordinate is present as
well (w was deleted); otherwise its eta value is at most N-3. Its
minimum eta value is at least -(N-3), so the same upper inequality
holds for all negative vertices. Thus eta·x<=N-3 is valid.

It is a facet: equality holds at e_a, at w+e_j for every one of the
N-3 remaining coordinates j, and at -sum_j e_j. These N-1 actual
vertices are affinely independent. Relative to e_a the first N-3
differences are e_b+e_j and the last is -e_a-sum_j e_j. Its a coordinate
forces the last coefficient in any relation to vanish, then each j
coordinate forces the other coefficients to vanish. The normal is
primitive because one coordinate equals 1.

For every N>=5 the dual vertex eta/(N-3) is not integral in the dual
lattice Z^(N-1): its b coordinate is 1/(N-3). These actual OMC polytopes
are therefore nonreflexive. At N=5 the exact h* row (1,24,64,26,1)
is also nonsymmetric, so it cannot have a gamma expansion symmetric
with respect to degree four. The target LC inequalities still hold.

The failure is broader than A2's unsuccessful numerical construction:
an explicit primitive facet disproves universal reflexivity in every
N>=5. It also differs from A1's zonotope reduction: the triangular
rank-one example there is reflexive and its binomial-square row is
symmetric; here the obstruction is the dual lattice and reciprocity.
One shared N5 calibration is disclosed and counts no extra trial.
[CIRCUIT-A3-CHECK](CIRCUIT-A3-CHECK.json) verifies exact facet/rank witnesses
at N=4..10; the argument above proves the unbounded statement.

The full conjecture needs a coefficient method accommodating
nonreflexive, nonsymmetric numerators. This is one failed uniform
reflexive reciprocal certificate, not a disproof of Q3678.

## A4: reduce to connected components by multiplying their numerators

The source's direct-sum identity makes a circuit polytope a free sum
P⊕Q=conv((P,0),(0,Q)). Try reducing the full conjecture to connected
oriented matroids by h*_(P⊕Q)=h*_P h*_Q; convolution would preserve
log-concavity and interval support. This multiplicativity fails on an
actual direct sum, even though both factors and the resulting row pass
the desired inequalities.

### Exact fractional-carry reduction

For any lattice polytope P with zero in its relative interior, let
g_P(z)=inf{lambda>=0: z in lambda P} on its saturated span lattice.
For separate lattices,

    (z,w) in m(P⊕Q) iff g_P(z)+g_Q(w)<=m.

This follows by distributing the convex weights between the two factors;
zero allows unused weight. The integer shell counts
s_P(k)=#{z: ceil(g_P(z))=k}=L_P(k)-L_P(k-1) have generating series
(1-t)E_P(t), with L_P(-1)=0. Thus counting only pairs satisfying
ceil(g_P)+ceil(g_Q)<=m predicts

    E_pred(t)=(1-t)E_P(t)E_Q(t),

whose numerator is h*_P h*_Q at the summed dimension. It counts a
subset of the actual lattice points. The exact discrepancy at m is

    D_m=#{(z,w): g_P,g_Q nonintegral,
          floor(g_P)+floor(g_Q)=m-1,
          {g_P}+{g_Q}<=1}.

To check this, if one gauge is integral there is no discrepancy.
Otherwise write their fractional parts f,g in (0,1). The actual minimal
integer dilate is a+b+ceil(f+g), whereas the predicted one is a+b+2.
They differ at exactly m=a+b+1 when f+g<=1. Therefore

    h*_(P⊕Q)=h*_P h*_Q+(1-t)^(dimP+dimQ+1) sum_m D_m t^m.

This is an exact all-size reduction, not a positive coefficient
convolution: finite differences of the nonnegative D_m can have both
signs. In particular integer gauges on one factor suffice for
multiplicativity, including reflexive P or Q with primitive facet
right sides one. The unrestricted direct-sum inference needs more.

### Actual failure of the product formula

Take P from the dual graphical matroid of K_5 minus one edge in A2.
The complete exact facet list has right sides one or two. The deleted
vertex w=(1,1,0,0) has gauge 3/2, as does -w: the new distance-two
facets give 3/2, and the remaining inequalities give at most that.
Both gauge values are nonintegral. Consequently each of the four pairs
(±w,±w) lies in 3(P⊕P), but neither factor can be placed into integer
dilates with total at most three.

There are no other carry pairs at m=3. Each nonzero integer point has
gauge at least one, since P lies in [-1,1]^4. Fractional gauges have
denominator two. The only integer points of gauge 3/2 are ±w: a point
at gauge at most 3/2 has coordinates in {-1,0,1}; the original complete-
graph range inequality precludes mixed +1,-1 coordinates. Thus it is
a positive or negative zero-one vertex of that complete polytope, and
every such point except ±w is already in P with gauge one.

It follows that D_0=D_1=D_2=0,D_3=4. Already the third h* coefficient
is 3128 instead of the product's 3124. The independent full counting
in [CIRCUIT-A4-CHECK](CIRCUIT-A4-CHECK.json) gives

    h*_(P⊕P)=(1,48,704,3128,5350,3372,800,52,1),
    h*_P²   =(1,48,704,3124,5346,3376,804,52,1).

The actual row is log-concave at every index. This is a genuine circuit
polytope of an 18-element oriented matroid, in dimension eight. Its
lattice is the product of the two saturated anchored-potential
lattices. Gauge-shell enumeration counts all integer pairs through
dimension eight; no rational lattice or rounded hull is used.

The failure is the integer splitting step of component multiplication,
distinct from A1's geometry or A3's reflexive certificate. It uses the
same nonreflexive factor as A3 but supplies an independent direct-sum
inequality mechanism and an exact all-size fractional-carry identity.
To finish Q3678 by components one must control that correction's
adjacent coefficient inequalities, not merely multiply the factor rows.
No such general compensation theorem was obtained. This is one
failed full-target component reduction, with its carry formula retained.

## A5: identify signed-circuit polytopes with a realization's flow lattice

For a totally unimodular representation A, a primitive integer circuit
dependence has coefficients in {0,+1,-1}; hence its sign vector really
lies in ker(A). This suggests a full-target route through flow-space
lattice methods: extend that identification to arbitrary realizations,
then use the matrix rank/corank and conformal circulation decomposition
to organize the Ehrhart counts. The identification is false even for
the simplest nonregular realizable uniform matroids, in every ground-set
size N>=4. Nonrepresentable oriented matroids would require a further
step as well; the proposed first step already fails on represented ones.

Let A_N have columns (1,j), j=1,...,N. It has rank two and all pairs
of columns independent. On i<j<k its unique minimal dependence has
coefficients (k-j, -(k-i), j-i). Its normalized sign circuit is

    c_(ijk)=e_i-e_j+e_k.

The first row of A_N sums this vector to 1, so it is not in the actual
kernel. This is not fixed by using a different absolute scaling of
the same dependence: discarding its magnitudes is exactly what the
target construction does. In fact the OMC polytope has full dimension
N, whereas every flow kernel of a rank-two realization has dimension
N-2.

To prove full dimension and the lattice statement simultaneously, use
the first four circuits

    c_123=(1,-1,1,0), c_124=(1,-1,0,1),
    c_134=(1,0,-1,1), c_234=(0,1,-1,1).

Their matrix determinant is 1. At general N add c_(12j) for j=5,...,N.
The resulting block matrix has that four-by-four upper block, an identity
block on the remaining coordinates, and a zero off-diagonal upper block.
Its determinant is still 1. Thus these circuits form a unimodular basis
of Z^N, and the actual polytope spans R^N. Its saturated lattice and its
vertex-generated lattice agree with Z^N; the obstruction cannot be
explained away as an index error or a rational-coordinate convention.

At N=4 these four circuits and their opposites are the entire circuit
set. Their polytope is a unimodular four-dimensional crosspolytope, with
h*=(1+t)^4, so the target inequality holds. That small known-catalog
calibration counts zero extra attempts. At larger N more circuits occur,
but the displayed unimodular basis and dimension obstruction persist.

This attempted flow-space transfer differs from A1's geometric zonotope
reduction and A3's dual-lattice reflexivity reduction: it fails at the
linear conservation equations of a nonregular realization. It also
differs from A4's carry correction for exact circuit polytopes already
constructed. An extension would have to study the span of **sign**
circuits in its own right, not substitute the realization's relation
space. No general Ehrhart coefficient theorem for that larger space
was obtained. This is one fifth failed full-target route.
