## A2 — solve all orderings in an equal-size join family

The construction realizes descending order in every degree, with explicit
s>=2(d-1)+1, and ascending order at s1. But a root-count comparison bound
limits its possible strict orders to (d-1)binom(floor(d/2),2)+1,
less than the required factorial for d>=12. This developed one-parameter
family cannot solve the arbitrary-permutation target. The all-degree
descending partial does not reset the full-target counter.

## A5 — assemble prescribed entries by connected sums

blocks through their connected sum, rather than convolving them as in A3.
For two d-variable quadratic complete intersections, every d>=3, the sum
requires a genuinely new degree-d relation identifying the socles.
It stays Artinian Gorenstein but is not quadratic or Koszul. The actual
minimal-relation proof defeats the needed assembly closure. Unlike A1
this is an explicit all-degree operation on already Koszul inputs, not
the topology of an approximate existence certificate.
