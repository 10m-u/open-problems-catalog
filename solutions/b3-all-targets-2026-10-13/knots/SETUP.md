This last assertion quantifies over every convex relation between support points, rather than only coordinate-neighbor inequalities. Theorem 1.2 reports all 51,280,976 alternating knots through 19 crossings verified; replay inside that range is validation, not fresh solve coverage. Type-B log concavity is stronger and already has actual exceptions.
* [Su–Xie–Yu v2, Conjecture 2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.27888v2): the Z/2-graded ruling polynomial of the **rainbow closure of any positive braid** has normalized form z^b R(z)=sum a_j z^(2j), where b is the number of components. The row (a_j) is log-concave with no internal zeros. Remark 2.5 expressly excludes unrestricted Legendrian links and reports positive braids on at most six strands and length at most 15 checked. Definition 2.1 and (2.1) give a finite permutation-walk formula.
* [Boninger v1, Conjectures 1.6 and 1.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.28484v1): for every oriented alternating link, the **Laurent** polynomial P_K(x,y,z,w) has M-convex support in Z⁴ (Q3626), and c_alpha² >= c_(alpha+e_i-e_j) c_(alpha-e_i+e_j) for every integer alpha and every i,j (Q3627). Missing coefficients are zero. P is homogeneous of signed total degree sigma(K), not an ordinary nonnegative-degree polynomial. Multiplying by a fixed monomial preserves both target conditions.

Positive edges form directed cycles; negative edges form directed cuts (Lemma 5.4). These restrictions cannot be dropped when a relaxed matching counterexample is used.
