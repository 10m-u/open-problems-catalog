# Q3617 — A three-variable mask with no algebra realization

**Proposed counterexample over every field; reconstructed research checkpoint, 2026-10-10.** The argument below is reconstructed from the earlier work, not a recovered original file or an externally reviewed result. Original exhaustive checker programs are absent; see [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md).

## Source and formulation

The recorded source is Luca Fiorindo, *Perazzo algebras, strongly Koszulness, Arithmetic Complexes, and Vasconcelos invariant*, Genova, 2026, Definition 2.13 and Question 2.16, printed pp. 44–45: [thesis PDF](https://unige.iris.cineca.it/retrieve/9b01309f-fa18-40af-87dd-aae58f1137f2/phdunige_5530713.pdf). This reference is carried forward from the prior investigation.

For `A` contained in `X=[n]` and `k` outside `A`, a mask `M(A,k)` is required to satisfy extensivity, monotonicity in `A`, symmetry

\[
j\in M(A,k)\iff k\in M(A,j),
\]

and the successive-colon identity

\[
M(M(A,k),j)=M(M(A,j),k)\quad\text{when }j\notin M(A,k).
\]

There must also be formal series with nonnegative integer coefficients satisfying

\[
F_X=1,\qquad F_A=F_{A\cup\{k\}}+tF_{M(A,k)}. \tag{1}
\]

The realizability question asks for a standard graded commutative algebra with a basis `x_1,...,x_n` of degree one such that, for the ideal `J_A=(x_i:i in A)`,

\[
(J_A:x_k)=J_{M(A,k)}.
\]

## Complete mask

Take `X={1,2,3}` and define all twelve values as follows.

| A | k | M(A,k) |
|---|---:|---|
| empty | 1 | empty |
| empty | 2 | empty |
| empty | 3 | empty |
| {1} | 2 | {1,2} |
| {1} | 3 | {1,3} |
| {2} | 1 | {2,3} |
| {2} | 3 | {1,2} |
| {3} | 1 | {2,3} |
| {3} | 2 | {1,3} |
| {1,2} | 3 | X |
| {1,3} | 2 | X |
| {2,3} | 1 | X |

Set

\[
F_X=1,\qquad F_A=1+t\ (|A|=2),\qquad
F_A=(1+t)^2\ (|A|=1),\qquad
F_\varnothing=\frac{(1+t)^2}{1-t}. \tag{2}
\]

## Verification of the formal conditions

Extensivity is immediate from the table. For a fixed `k`, a proper increase of `A` takes an empty value to a two-element value, or a two-element value to `X`, proving monotonicity.

For symmetry, at `A=empty` both memberships are false. At `A={1}`, with distinct available `j,k`, both are false. At `A={2}` or `{3}`, both are true. Cases with `j=k` are tautological, and larger `A` has at most one available index.

For the successive-colon identity, at `A=empty` both sides are empty. At `A={1}` and distinct available indices, both sides are `X`. At `A={2}` or `{3}`, the only admissible case has `j=k`, so equality is automatic. At a two-element `A` there is no `j` outside `M(A,k)=X`.

The series have nonnegative integer coefficients. Their recurrences reduce to

\[
1+t=1+t\cdot1,\qquad
(1+t)^2=(1+t)+t(1+t),\qquad
\frac{(1+t)^2}{1-t}=(1+t)^2+t\frac{(1+t)^2}{1-t}.
\]

Thus all the required combinatorial and formal-series conditions hold.

## Degree-two obstruction

Suppose a realizing algebra exists over a field `K`. Write

\[
R=K[x,y,z]/I,\qquad I_1=0,
\]

with `x,y,z` corresponding to indices `1,2,3`. The colon exact sequences force the Hilbert recurrences (1). Starting from the three-variable quotient, they determine the Hilbert series in (2): first the two-element subsets, then the singletons, and finally the empty set by division by `1-t`. In particular, `dim_K R_2=4` and `dim_K I_2=6-4=2`.

The entries `M({1},2)={1,2}` and `M({1},3)={1,3}` imply `y^2,z^2` lie in `xR_1`. Thus `I_2` contains independent quadrics

\[
f=y^2-x(ax+by+cz),\qquad
g=z^2-x(dx+ey+rz), \tag{3}
\]

for scalars `a,b,c,d,e,r` in `K`. They form a basis because `dim I_2=2`.

The entry `M({2},1)={2,3}` implies `xz` lies in `yR_1`. For some linear form `ell` and scalars `u,v`,

\[
xz-y\ell=uf+vg.
\]

Comparison of the `z^2` coefficient gives `v=0`. Comparison of the `xz` coefficient gives `-uc=1`, hence `u` is nonzero. The `x^2` coefficient now gives `-ua=0`, so `a=0`.

Likewise, `M({3},1)={2,3}` implies `xy` lies in `zR_1`. Write

\[
xy-zm=u'f+v'g
\]

for a linear form `m`. Comparing `y^2` gives `u'=0`, comparing `xy` gives `-v'e=1`, and comparing `x^2` gives `d=0`.

Consequently every element of `I_2` has zero `x^2` coefficient. But `M({2,3},1)=X` implies `x^2` lies in `yR_1+zR_1`. This produces an element

\[
x^2-y\ell'-zm'\in I_2
\]

whose `x^2` coefficient is one, a contradiction.

The argument works in every characteristic, including two. It uses no assumption that the entire defining ideal is generated by quadrics. If the source formulation agrees with the one above, this mask gives a negative answer over every field.
