# Q3740 — Closed range of a semigroup increment implies closed range of its generator

**Published-resolution correction with a reconstructed proof; research checkpoint, 2026-10-10.** This is not presented as a new theorem. No exclusion or canonical status change is made by this checkpoint. See [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md) for provenance and missing artifacts.

## Problem and publication attribution

The catalog source is Madou, Remizov and Vafadar, [*Open problems in one-parameter operator semigroups theory*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00416v2), Problem 25, printed p. 19, attributed there to Yuri Latushkin (2003).

For a strongly continuous semigroup `T(t)` on a Banach space `X`, with generator `A`, the question is whether closedness of `ran(I-T(t_0))` for some `t_0>0` implies closedness of `ran A`.

The earlier source investigation identified Sebastian Król, [*A complement to spectral mapping theorems for C₀-semigroups*](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms/bdq076), Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society 43(1) (2011), 17–25, published online 29 October 2010, as a published resolution. The recorded publisher abstract includes the implication and a statement for powers. **Only the abstract and publication metadata were inspected in that investigation; the paid full text was not read.** This note does not attribute the particular proof below to the unread paper. The bibliographic attribution should be rechecked before integrating a catalog exclusion.

## Reconstructed proof of the stated implication

Fix `t>0` such that `B=T(t)-I` has closed range. Introduce the bounded operators

\[
S=\frac1t\int_0^tT(s)\,ds,\qquad
U=\frac1t\int_0^t(t-s)T(s)\,ds.
\]

The integrals act on each vector in the strong sense. Local boundedness of the semigroup makes both operators bounded. The standard generator integral identity gives

\[
SX\subseteq D(A),\qquad AS=B/t. \tag{1}
\]

For `x` in `D(A)`, integration of the derivative of `T(s)x` gives

\[
SAx=Bx/t,\qquad UAx=Sx-x. \tag{2}
\]

In particular, `S(ker B)` is contained in `ker A`; it is not necessary to identify the two kernels.

Since the bounded operator `B` has closed range, its induced injective map from `X/ker B` onto `ran B` has bounded inverse. Hence some `c>0` satisfies

\[
\operatorname{dist}(x,\ker B)\le c\|Bx\|\qquad(x\in X). \tag{3}
\]

For `x` in `D(A)`, use (1)–(3) to estimate

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{dist}(x,\ker A)
&\le\|x-Sx\|+\operatorname{dist}(Sx,\ker A)\\
&\le\|U\|\,\|Ax\|+\|S\|\operatorname{dist}(x,\ker B)\\
&\le\bigl(\|U\|+ct\|S\|^2\bigr)\|Ax\|. \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
\]

For clarity, (4) implies closedness of the range even though `A` is unbounded. Its kernel is closed in `X`, because `A` is closed. Define on `ran A` the inverse-quotient map

\[
J(Ax)=x+\ker A\in X/\ker A.
\]

Inequality (4) says this well-defined linear map is bounded. If `y_n=Ax_n` converges in `X`, then `J(y_n)` converges in the Banach space `X/ker A`, say to `x+ker A`. Choose `k_n` in `ker A` so that `x_n-k_n` converges to `x`; this is possible by the definition of convergence in quotient norm. Since `A(x_n-k_n)=y_n` converges and `A` is closed, `x` belongs to `D(A)` and `Ax=lim y_n`. Thus the limit belongs to `ran A`, proving that range is closed.

Replacing `B` by `-B` changes no range-closedness property, so this proves the stated implication for `I-T(t)`.

## The converse fails

On complex `ell^2` indexed by integers `n>=2`, let

\[
(T(t)x)_n=e^{2\pi i(n+1/n)t}x_n.
\]

This is a strongly continuous unitary group, with generator

\[
(Ax)_n=2\pi i(n+1/n)x_n
\]

on its maximal diagonal domain. The reciprocal diagonal is bounded and sends `ell^2` into that domain, so `A` has full range and a bounded inverse.

However, `T(1)-I` is the bounded diagonal operator with entries

\[
e^{2\pi i/n}-1,
\]

which are nonzero and tend to zero. It is injective but not bounded below, as seen on the unit coordinate vectors. An injective bounded operator with closed range must be bounded below. Therefore `ran(T(1)-I)` is not closed. The argument proves the one-way implication, not an equivalence.
