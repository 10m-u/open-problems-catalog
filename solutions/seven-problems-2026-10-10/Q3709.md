# Q3709 — Submultiplicativity at integer genus

**Proposed affirmative proof for integer h >= 1; reconstructed research checkpoint, 2026-10-10.** This is a written research argument awaiting mathematical review, not an external certification. Original computational checks and separate reviews are unavailable; see [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md).

## Source and claim

The recorded source is Schroeder and Hung P. Tong-Viet, *On the invariants of finite groups arising in a topological quantum field theory*, Pacific Journal of Mathematics 344 (2026), 151–182, Remark 1.8: [published PDF](https://msp.org/pjm/2026/344-1/pjm-v344-n1-p07-s.pdf), [preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14971v2). These references are carried forward from the earlier source inspection.

For a finite group `K`, let

\[
q_h(K)=\frac1{|K|}\sum_{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)}\chi(1)^{2-2h}.
\]

The proposed conclusion is

\[
q_h(G)\le q_h(N)q_h(G/N) \tag{1}
\]

for every finite group `G`, normal subgroup `N`, and **integer** `h>=1`. Put `s=2h-2` and

\[
\zeta_s(K)=\sum_{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)}\chi(1)^{-s}.
\]

Since group orders multiply, (1) is equivalent to `zeta_s(G)<=zeta_s(N)zeta_s(G/N)`.

## Lemma 1: subgroup comparison

If `H<=K` has index `t` and `s>=0` is real, then

\[
\zeta_s(H)\le t^{s+1}\zeta_s(K). \tag{2}
\]

For each `psi` in `Irr(H)`, choose one irreducible constituent `chi` of its induced character. Then `chi(1)<=t psi(1)`, and Frobenius reciprocity puts `psi` in the restriction of `chi`. The number of distinct constituents of that restriction is at most

\[
\langle\chi|_H,\chi|_H\rangle_H
=\frac1{|H|}\sum_{x\in H}|\chi(x)|^2
\le\frac1{|H|}\sum_{x\in K}|\chi(x)|^2=t.
\]

Thus at most `t` chosen `psi` map to any one `chi`, and each contributes at most `t^s chi(1)^{-s}`. Summing proves (2).

## Lemma 2: a central-character sector is no larger than the trivial sector

Let `E` be finite, `Z` a central subgroup, and `lambda` a linear character of `Z`. For `s=2h-2` with integer `h>=1`,

\[
\sum_{\chi:\,\chi|_Z=\chi(1)\lambda}\chi(1)^{-s}
\le\zeta_s(E/Z). \tag{3}
\]

Let `c_h(g)` count tuples in `E^{2h}` whose product of `h` commutators equals `g`. In particular `c_h(g)>=0`. The character expansion is

\[
c_h(g)=|E|^{2h-1}\sum_{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(E)}
\frac{\chi(g^{-1})}{\chi(1)^{2h-1}}. \tag{4}
\]

For completeness, let `C=sum_{a,b in E}[a,b]` in the complex group algebra. In a degree-`d` irreducible representation, `C` acts as `|E|^2/d^2` times the identity. This follows by averaging conjugations and matrix-coefficient orthogonality. Raising to the `h`th power and expanding the central idempotents

\[
e_\chi=\frac{d}{|E|}\sum_{g\in E}\chi(g^{-1})g
\]

gives (4) by taking the coefficient of `g`.

For central `z`, write `chi(z)=chi(1) lambda_chi(z)`. Orthogonality on `Z` gives

\[
\sum_{z\in Z}\lambda(z)c_h(z)
=|Z||E|^{2h-1}
\sum_{\chi:\lambda_\chi=\lambda}\chi(1)^{-s}.
\]

The right side is nonnegative real. By the triangle inequality and `c_h(z)>=0`, the left side is at most `sum_{z in Z} c_h(z)`. The latter corresponds to the trivial central character; its irreducibles are exactly those of `E/Z`. This proves (3). The integer-genus assumption is used in the nonnegative commutator count.

## Corollary: projective degree sums

For a fixed complex multiplier `alpha` on a finite group `K`, let `zeta_s^alpha(K)` be the sum of inverse `s`th powers of the dimensions of its irreducible projective representations. Then

\[
\zeta_s^\alpha(K)\le\zeta_s(K). \tag{5}
\]

To realize a finite central extension explicitly, normalize `alpha` and put `m=|K|`. The twisted regular operators satisfy `L_a L_b=alpha(a,b)L_{ab}`. Taking determinants yields

\[
\alpha(a,b)^m=\frac{\det L_a\det L_b}{\det L_{ab}}.
\]

Choose `m`th roots `u_a` of `det L_a`, with `u_1=1`, and replace the multiplier by

\[
\beta(a,b)=\alpha(a,b)\frac{u_{ab}}{u_au_b}.
\]

Its values belong to the finite group of `m`th roots of unity. The corresponding finite central extension `E=mu_m times_beta K` has a central-character sector whose representations are precisely the `beta`-projective representations, with the same dimensions. Cohomologous multipliers preserve dimensions. Applying (3) proves (5).

## Clifford-theoretic summation

Set `Q=G/N`. For each `G`-orbit in `Irr(N)`, choose `theta`. Let `I` be its inertia subgroup, put `K=I/N<=Q`, and let `t=[G:I]=[Q:K]`. Clifford theory identifies the irreducibles of `G` over this orbit with irreducible projective representations `rho` of `K` for a fixed multiplier, with degrees

\[
\chi(1)=t\,\theta(1)\rho(1).
\]

The contribution of this orbit to `zeta_s(G)` is therefore bounded, using (5) and then (2), by

\[
\begin{aligned}
t^{-s}\theta(1)^{-s}\zeta_s^\alpha(K)
&\le t^{-s}\theta(1)^{-s}\zeta_s(K)\\
&\le t^{-s}\theta(1)^{-s}t^{s+1}\zeta_s(Q)\\
&=t\theta(1)^{-s}\zeta_s(Q).
\end{aligned}
\]

The orbit contains exactly `t` characters, each of degree `theta(1)`. Summing over all orbits gives

\[
\zeta_s(G)\le\zeta_s(N)\zeta_s(Q),
\]

which proves the proposed inequality (1). Direct products give equality.
