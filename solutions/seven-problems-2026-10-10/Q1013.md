# Q1013 — Identification of stable causal AR(2) models

**Partial result; reconstructed research checkpoint, 2026-10-10.** This does not solve the general higher-order catalog conjecture. The original exact-algebra checker and separate review files are not included; see [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md).

## Source and precise scope

The recorded source is Marko Mlikota, [*Parameter Identification and Inference in Discretely Sampled or Temporally Aggregated Autoregressions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13224v2), Conjecture 1 and Proposition 5. The source's broader problem concerns arbitrary known order. This reconstructed argument treats only stable causal real Gaussian **AR(2)** models, periodic point sampling, nonzero roots, and distinct sampled powers. It makes no claim about the separate block-aggregation question Q1014. Source details are carried forward from the earlier investigation.

Consider

\[
x_t=\phi_1x_{t-1}+\phi_2x_{t-2}+e_t,\qquad e_t\overset{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,v),\quad v>0,
\]

with

\[
1-\phi_1z-\phi_2z^2=(1-az)(1-bz),\qquad
0<|a|,|b|<1,\quad a^q\ne b^q.
\]

For `y_n=x_{qn}`, the proposed conclusion is that its law determines `(phi_1,phi_2,v)` uniquely when `q` is odd, and determines exactly the pair

\[
(\phi_1,\phi_2,v),\qquad(-\phi_1,\phi_2,v)
\]

when `q` is even. Competitors may be any stable real Gaussian AR(2) models, including those with zero or repeated roots; these degenerate competitors will be excluded by the observed covariance form. Noncausal or unstable competitors are outside the statement. Four covariances suffice for reconstruction.

## Covariance residues

Write `gamma_k=Cov(x_0,x_k)`. Yule--Walker gives

\[
\frac{\gamma_1}{\gamma_0}=\frac{a+b}{1+ab}.
\]

The recurrence then gives, for `k>=0`,

\[
\gamma_k=C_a a^k+C_b b^k,
\quad
\frac{C_a}{\gamma_0}=\frac{a(1-b^2)}{(1+ab)(a-b)},
\quad
\frac{C_b}{\gamma_0}=\frac{b(1-a^2)}{(1+ab)(b-a)}. \tag{1}
\]

Both residues are nonzero. With `mu=a^q`, `nu=b^q` and `c_n=Cov(y_0,y_n)`,

\[
c_n=C_a\mu^n+C_b\nu^n. \tag{2}
\]

Its generating function has two distinct, noncancelled simple poles. A competitor with a zero root, coincident sampled powers, or a repeated original root has at most one nonzero pole, possibly double in the last case. It therefore cannot produce (2). After matching the two poles, a nondegenerate competitor must match the two residues as well.

A real-root representation cannot be replaced by a conjugate pair: if a conjugate pair has real sampled powers, those powers coincide. That is excluded in (2).

## Conjugate-root aliases

For `a=rho exp(i theta)` and `b=conjugate(a)`, put

\[
\kappa=\frac{1-\rho^2}{1+\rho^2}>0.
\]

Equation (1) becomes

\[
\frac{\gamma_k}{\gamma_0}
=\rho^k\bigl(\cos(k\theta)+\kappa\cot\theta\sin(k\theta)\bigr),
\qquad
\frac{C_a}{\gamma_0}=\frac{1-i\kappa\cot\theta}{2}. \tag{3}
\]

Matching `mu` fixes `rho`. Matching `C_a` then forces equality of the cotangents. Thus the matched root can only be `a` or `-a`, with the conjugate root receiving the same sign. Odd `q` excludes the minus sign; even `q` permits it.

## Real-root aliases

For odd `q`, each real sampled power has a unique real `q`th root. For even `q`, only the two signs of each matched root are possible. A common sign reversal preserves (1). A mixed sign reversal, after equating the matched `C_a`, would imply

\[
(1+ab)(1-b/a)=(1-ab)(1+b/a).
\]

The difference between these sides is `2b(a^2-1)/a`, nonzero under the assumptions. Therefore mixed signs are impossible.

## Parameters and sufficiency

Recover

\[
\phi_1=a+b,\qquad \phi_2=-ab,
\]

and, again from Yule--Walker,

\[
v=\gamma_0\frac{1+\phi_2}{1-\phi_2}
\bigl((1-\phi_2)^2-\phi_1^2\bigr). \tag{4}
\]

The variance is invariant under `phi_1 -> -phi_1`. Conversely, replacing `x_t` by `(-1)^t x_t` reverses that coefficient and preserves the law of the innovations. For even `q` it leaves every sampled observation unchanged, proving that the two alternatives really are observationally equivalent. They are distinct: `phi_1=0` would imply `b=-a`, contradicting distinct sampled powers for even `q`.

## Reconstruction from four covariances

From (2),

\[
\Delta=c_0c_2-c_1^2=C_aC_b(\mu-\nu)^2\ne0.
\]

This remains valid when `c_1=0`. Set

\[
s=\frac{c_0c_3-c_1c_2}{\Delta}=\mu+\nu,
\qquad
d=\frac{c_1c_3-c_2^2}{\Delta}=\mu\nu.
\]

The roots of `z^2-sz+d` give the unordered sampled roots, and

\[
C_a=\frac{c_1-c_0\nu}{\mu-\nu},\qquad C_b=c_0-C_a.
\]

For conjugate roots, take `rho=|mu|^(1/q)` and

\[
u_0=-\frac{2\operatorname{Im}C_a}{c_0\kappa}=\cot\theta.
\]

The two candidates `a=+/- rho (u_0+i)/sqrt(1+u_0^2)` are filtered by `a^q=mu`. The companion root is its conjugate. For real roots, take real `q`th-root candidates and apply the residue test. Equations (4) and the coefficient formulas finish the reconstruction.

## Limits and verification boundary

Higher-order covariance residues depend on all the roots, so the AR(2) residue argument cannot simply be applied pairwise to AR(p). The general conjecture remains unresolved by this note. The earlier work reports finite exact real-root and algebraic alias checks, including a zero-lag-one covariance control, but the corresponding programs and certificates were not recovered as files and have not been rerun for this PR. The result requires review within its stated order-two, stable-causal scope.
