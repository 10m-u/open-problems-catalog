# Q3778 — Exact counting of connected vertex partitions

**Proposed #P-completeness proof under polynomial-time Turing reductions; reconstructed research checkpoint, 2026-10-10.** This concerns exact counting, not approximate counting or uniform sampling. Original verifiers and certificates are not included; see [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md).

## Source, definition, and hardness input

A connected partition is an **unordered** partition of the vertices into nonempty blocks, each inducing a connected subgraph. The number of blocks is unrestricted. Write `C(G)` for the number of such partitions.

The recorded catalog source is Elle Najt's 2021 Wisconsin thesis, *Connected graph partitions from perspectives of complexity theory, statistical physics and probability theory, with applications to ensemble analysis of political redistricting plans and other statistical models*, Question 4.1.2, printed p. 82: [PDF](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/L3OLMTH4ESXZN8T/R/file-19c5d.pdf). The earlier investigation also consulted Simon–Tittmann–Trinks, [*Counting Connected Set Partitions of Graphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.1726); a hardness result for a bivariate refinement does not alone settle its unweighted specialization.

The external hardness input used here is exact counting of spanning connected edge subsets, equivalently `T_G(1,2)` for connected `G`. The recorded references are Jaeger–Vertigan–Welsh, [*On the computational complexity of the Jones and Tutte polynomials*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100068936) (1990), Theorem 2 and equation (2.6), and [Provan–Ball (1983)](https://doi.org/10.1137/0212053). These references are carried forward from the original investigation, not a new source check.

The proposed conclusion is that computing `C(G)` is #P-complete under polynomial-time Turing reductions, already for connected bipartite simple graphs. No parsimonious or many-one reduction is claimed.

## An auxiliary polynomial

For a loopless graph `G=(V,E)`, put `m=|E|`. For `A` contained in `E`, define

\[
\operatorname{cl}_G(A)=\{uv\in E:u,v\text{ lie in one component of }(V,A)\}.
\]

Set

\[
P_G(x,y)=\sum_{A\subseteq E}
x^{|\operatorname{cl}_G(A)|-|A|}y^{m-|\operatorname{cl}_G(A)|}. \tag{1}
\]

All exponents are nonnegative, and total degree is at most `m`. When `G` is connected,

\[
P_G(1,0)=\#\{A\subseteq E:(V,A)\text{ is connected}\}, \tag{2}
\]

where polynomial evaluation uses `0^0=1`. Indeed, a term survives exactly when every original edge has endpoints in one `A`-component. Connectivity of `G` then forces a single `A`-component.

## What subdivision counts

Let `G^(ell)` replace each original edge by a path of `ell` edges, with fresh internal vertices distinct across paths. The key identity is

\[
C(G^{(\ell)})=P_G(2^\ell-\ell-1,\,2^\ell-1),\qquad\ell\ge1. \tag{3}
\]

To prove it, start with a connected vertex partition of the subdivided graph. Let `A` consist of original edges whose whole replacement path lies in one block. Two original vertices are in one block exactly when they are connected in `(V,A)`. For the nontrivial direction, a simple path inside that block between original vertices must traverse whole replacement paths between consecutive original vertices, because internal vertices have degree two.

On each replacement path, mark a cut between consecutive vertices assigned to different blocks. There are `ell` possible cut positions. An edge in `A` has no cuts, giving one state. An edge in `cl_G(A)` but not `A` has endpoints in the same block but an interrupted path. Exactly one cut is impossible: its endpoint segments would have the same block label, so it would not be a cut. Any cut set of size at least two is possible, giving `2^ell-ell-1` states. The first and last segments join the endpoints' common block; strictly internal segments are separate blocks. An edge outside `cl_G(A)` has endpoints in different blocks, and every nonempty cut set is possible, giving `2^ell-1` states.

A block without an original vertex cannot leave one replacement path or occupy two disjoint intervals on it: a connecting route would pass through an original vertex or another block. Thus these are all the local states. Conversely, fix `A`, use its components for the original-vertex blocks, and make the stated choices independently on each replacement path. Whole paths in `A` connect every original-vertex block, endpoint segments attach to it, and other blocks are intervals. This inverts the extraction of `A`. Multiplying the local counts and summing proves (3).

## Polynomial-size interpolation

A rational polynomial `P(x,y)` of total degree at most `m` is determined by

\[
P(4^j-2j-1,\,4^j-1),\qquad j=1,\ldots,D,
\quad D=\frac{(m+1)(m+2)}2. \tag{4}
\]

For uniqueness, make the invertible affine substitution

\[
Q(t,z)=P(z-t-1,z-1)=\sum_{b=0}^m q_b(t)z^b,
\qquad\deg q_b\le m-b.
\]

The evaluation sequence is

\[
f(j)=\sum_{b=0}^m q_b(2j)(4^b)^j. \tag{5}
\]

With `E` the forward-shift operator, `(E-r)` lowers the polynomial factor's degree in a sequence `p(j)r^j`. Therefore (5) is annihilated by the monic recurrence

\[
L(E)=\prod_{b=0}^m(E-4^b)^{m-b+1},
\]

of order `D`. If its first `D` values vanish, all subsequent values vanish. Choose the largest `b` with `q_b` nonzero. Dividing (5) by `(4^b)^j` would then force the polynomial `q_b(2j)` to tend to zero, since all lower-base terms decay exponentially times a polynomial. That is impossible for a nonzero polynomial. Thus every `q_b` is zero, proving uniqueness.

In the monomial basis `t^a z^b`, `a+b<=m`, the evaluation matrix has entries

\[
(2j)^a4^{bj},\qquad1\le j\le D.
\]

Their bit lengths are `O(mD+m log D)=O(m^3)`. There are `O(m^2)` unknowns. Determinant bounds and exact rational or fraction-free elimination give polynomial bit complexity when the supplied evaluation values have polynomial bit length. Finally,

\[
P(1,0)=Q(-1,1). \tag{6}
\]

## Reduction and input sizes

Given connected `G` with `m` edges, query a connected-partition oracle on `G^(2j)` for `j=1,...,D`. Identity (3) supplies the evaluations (4) of `P_G`. Interpolate and apply (6) to recover the spanning connected-edge-subset count (2).

Each query graph has

\[
|V(G^{(2j)})|=|V|+m(2j-1),\qquad |E(G^{(2j)})|=2jm,
\]

so its size is `O(|V|+m^3)`. A graph on `s` vertices has at most `s^s` partitions, making every oracle answer's binary length `O(s log s)`. This accounts for arithmetic as well as query size. All calls are nonadaptive.

Every even subdivision is connected and bipartite: assign the same color to all original vertices and alternate along each even-length replacement path. For simple original graphs the queries are simple. The hardness input may also be taken on multigraphs: remove loops, whose independent choices contribute a known factor of two each. The proof of (3) applies to parallel edges, while subdivisions of length at least two replace them by internally disjoint paths and yield simple query graphs. For disconnected inputs, compute the spanning-set count componentwise and multiply. This accommodates the graphic-matroid formulation of the cited hardness theorem without silently assuming a stronger restriction on its input graphs.

## Membership in #P

Fix an ordering of the vertices. Encode an unordered partition by its unique restricted-growth string: `a_1=1` and `1<=a_i<=1+max_{j<i} a_j`. Equal labels define the blocks. A polynomial-time verifier checks the canonical encoding and connectivity within each block. There is exactly one accepted certificate per connected partition. Thus `C(G)` belongs to #P, completing the proposed Turing-completeness classification.

## Verification boundary

The earlier work describes independent small-graph partition enumeration, comparisons with the edge-subset polynomial, and exact interpolation controls on paths and cycles. For example, a triangle has

\[
P_{C_3}(x,y)=y^3+3y^2+3x+1,
\]

whose value at `(1,0)` is four, and a cycle of length `r>=3` has `2^r-r` connected partitions. The original checker and certificate files have not been recovered or rerun here. Finite controls do not establish the general reduction or certify novelty. The subdivision bijection, interpolation, and applicability of the external hardness theorem require review before catalog integration.
