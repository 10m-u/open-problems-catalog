# Q187 — Uniform concentration for Ferrers-board rook profiles

**Proposed affirmative argument; reconstructed research checkpoint, 2026-10-10.** This note preserves the mathematical argument available from the earlier work. It is not a peer-reviewed result or a novelty certification. The original finite verifiers and certificates are not included; see [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md).

## Source and statement

The recorded source is Pakawut Jiradilok, [*Large-scale Rook Placements*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00615), Conjecture 5.9, also Conjecture 3.4.9 of his 2022 MIT thesis. The argument uses the source's marginal dilation identity, Proposition 5.6, and its limiting expected profile. Source references are carried forward from the earlier investigation, not a new literature search.

Fix a partition with `n = lambda_1 >= ... >= lambda_n > 0` and `lambda_i >= n+1-i`. Dilate every cell by an `N` by `N` block. Put `M=nN`, choose uniformly a full rook placement, and represent it by a permutation `pi` of the columns. With the original matrix row labels, let

\[
X_N(t)=\frac1M\sum_{i=1}^M\mathbf1\{i+\pi(i)\le Mt\},\qquad 0\le t\le2.
\]

Let `m_lambda` be the limiting expected profile defined in the source. The proposed quantitative conclusion is, for every `u>0`,

\[
\Pr\left(\sup_{0\le t\le2}|X_N(t)-m_\lambda(t)|>u+2/N\right)
\le 2(2nN+1)\exp(-nNu^2/2). \tag{1}
\]

This implies uniform convergence in probability. For placements realized together on any probability space, it also implies almost-sure convergence, without independence across `N`.

## Conditional swap coupling

Order the rows for revelation as `r_1,...,r_M` so that their allowed column sets satisfy

\[
R_1\subseteq R_2\subseteq\cdots\subseteq R_M.
\]

This changes only the revelation order, not the labels in the statistic. Fix a feasible prefix through row `r_{j-1}`. For two unused columns `a,b` allowed in row `r_j`, take a completion with `pi(r_j)=a`. Column `b` occurs in a unique later row `r_h`. Interchange these assignments. The result is legal because `a` belongs to `R_j`, hence to `R_h`; the prefix is unchanged. Interchanging again is the inverse. Thus this is a bijection coupling the uniform conditional completions for choices `a` and `b`.

Every unused allowed column is feasible: start from any completion and apply this swap to its current column and the desired one.

## Unit change of an antidiagonal count

Write

\[
F_k(\pi)=\sum_i\mathbf1\{i+\pi(i)\le k\}.
\]

A swap involving two rows `r<s` and columns `a<b` changes the affected sums between the pairs `(r+a,s+b)` and `(r+b,s+a)`. The first pair contains the smallest and largest of the four numbers. If its contribution to `F_k` is zero or two, the second pair has the same contribution. If it contributes one, the second pair contributes zero, one, or two. Consequently every such swap satisfies

\[
|F_k(\pi)-F_k(\pi')|\le1. \tag{2}
\]

## Martingale concentration

For fixed `k`, expose the placement in the nested row order and set

\[
Z_j=\mathbb E[F_k\mid\pi(r_1),\ldots,\pi(r_j)].
\]

Given a prefix of length `j-1`, the possible next conditional expectations differ pairwise by at most one, by the coupling and (2). Their conditional average is `Z_{j-1}`, so `|Z_j-Z_{j-1}|<=1`.

For a conditionally mean-zero random variable `D` with `|D|<=1`, convexity gives

\[
\mathbb E[e^{\theta D}\mid\mathcal F]\le\cosh\theta\le e^{\theta^2/2}.
\]

Iterating over the martingale differences and optimizing the exponential Markov bound gives

\[
\Pr(|F_k-\mathbb EF_k|\ge s)\le2e^{-s^2/(2M)}.
\]

Both `X_N(t)` and its expectation are constant between consecutive multiples of `1/M`. A union bound over `k=0,...,2M` therefore yields

\[
\Pr\left(\sup_t|X_N(t)-\mathbb EX_N(t)|\ge u\right)
\le2(2M+1)e^{-Mu^2/2}. \tag{3}
\]

## Uniform approximation of the mean

Let `p_ab` be the probability of a rook in macrocell `(a,b)` of a uniform full placement on the undilated board, extended by zero outside the board. Then `sum p_ab=n`. For independent uniform `U,V` on `(0,1)`, set

\[
c(s)=\Pr(U+V\le s),\qquad
c_N(s)=\Pr((\lceil NU\rceil+\lceil NV\rceil)/N\le s).
\]

The density of `U+V` is at most one, and rounding increases its value by at most `2/N`. Hence

\[
c(s-2/N)\le c_N(s)\le c(s),\qquad \sup_s|c_N(s)-c(s)|\le2/N.
\]

The source's marginal identity assigns probability `p_ab/N` to each specified microscopic cell in macrocell `(a,b)`. Summing below the antidiagonal gives

\[
\mathbb EX_N(t)=\frac1n\sum_{a,b}p_{ab}c_N(nt-a-b+2),
\qquad
m_\lambda(t)=\frac1n\sum_{a,b}p_{ab}c(nt-a-b+2).
\]

The weights sum to one, so `sup_t |E X_N(t)-m_lambda(t)| <= 2/N`. Combining this with (3) proves (1). For a fixed positive tolerance the resulting tail probabilities are summable in `N`. The first Borel--Cantelli lemma, applied to a countable sequence of tolerances tending to zero, gives the almost-sure statement.
