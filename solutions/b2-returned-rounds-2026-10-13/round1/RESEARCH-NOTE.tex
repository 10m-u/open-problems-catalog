\begin{abstract}
For an admissible partition $\Fp^n=A_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup A_k\sqcup R$, where
$p$ is an odd prime and no pair in a single $A_i$ has midpoint in $R$, a
positive-density remainder contains an affine subspace of codimension
$O_p(k\log(2k))$. More generally, density at least $\alpha>0$ gives codimension
$O_p(k(\log(2k)+\log(1/\alpha)))$. For $p=3$ and $\alpha=1/10$, this improves
the $O(k^2)$ bound in the primary source underlying Q4296.
The proof combines the established polynomial Freiman--Ruzsa covering theorem
with a density-preserving quotient-avoidance lemma. An explicit block
construction has largest affine subspace of dimension exactly $n-k$ while
its remainder has density at least $9/10$, showing that the upper bound is
within a logarithmic factor of the necessary dependence on $k$ in the stated
ambient-dimension range. Separately, exact rational logarithm certificates
for Q4660 give a finite integer law with entropy-increment ratio strictly
larger than $1.40518$, improving the supplied handoff's witness near $1.39778$.
Neither result determines the exact extremal function or the optimal
universal entropy constant.
\end{abstract}

\section{An explicit covering-to-subspace transfer theorem}

All logarithms in the combinatorial proof are natural unless their base is
shown. An affine subspace is nonempty. Empty colour classes are allowed in
intermediate restrictions; this only strengthens applicability to the
original partition problem.

Fix an odd prime $p$. Let $M_p(k)$ be any positive integer such that every
nonempty set $A$ in a finite-dimensional $\Fp$-vector space with
$|A+A|\leq k|A|$ is contained in at most $M_p(k)$ cosets of a subspace $H$
with $|H|\leq |A|$. The established PFR theorem provides an absolute constant
$C_0$ for which one may take
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coverfunction}
 M_p(k)=\left\lceil(2k)^{C_0p^3}\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
This is the covering form of Theorem 1.1 in \cite{pfr}, not a conjectural
polynomial Bogolyubov statement.

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative transfer]\label{thm:transfer}
Let $k\geq1$, $0<\alpha\leq1$, and suppose
\[
 \Fp^n=A_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup A_k\sqcup R,\qquad
 |R|\geq\alpha p^n,
\]
with $(x+y)/2\notin R$ for every $x,y\in A_i$ and every $i$.
Put
\begin{align}
 M&=M_p(k), &
 s&=\left\lceil\log_p(2kM)\right\rceil,\label{eq:s}\\
 \tau&=\frac{1}{4p^s}, &
 T&=\left\lceil(2k+1)\log\frac{4p^s}{\alpha}\right\rceil.
 \label{eq:tauT}
\end{align}
Then $R$ contains an affine subspace of codimension at most
\[
 \min\{n,T+ks\}.
\]
In particular, for each fixed odd prime $p$ the codimension is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mainasymptotic}
 O_p\!\left(k\left[\log(2k)+\log(1/\alpha)\right]\right).
\end{equation}
Using the $p$-dependence in \eqref{eq:coverfunction}, an absolute implied
constant gives the bound
$O(k[p^3\log(2k)+\log(1/\alpha)])$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Q4296]\label{cor:q4296}
There is an absolute constant $C$ such that every admissible partition in
Q4296 has an affine subspace $V\subseteq R$ satisfying
\[
 \dim V\geq \max\{0,n-Ck\log(2k)\}.
\]
Thus the established quadratic codimension loss can be replaced by a
logarithmic overhead over linear dependence on the number of colours.
\end{corollary}

The essential distinction is between covering a colour by polynomially
many cosets and enclosing it in one affine span. The former can be avoided
at low codimension while preserving the density of the other colours.
The following lemma supplies that preservation.

\subsection{Density-preserving avoidance in a quotient}

\begin{lemma}[Weighted avoidance]\label{lem:avoidance}
Let $W$ be an $\Fp$-vector space, $H\leq W$, and suppose that $A\subseteq W$
is contained in at most $M$ cosets of $H$. Let
$1\leq s\leq\dim(W/H)$ and $Mp^{-s}<1$. For every function
$f:W\longrightarrow[0,\infty)$ there is an affine subspace $W'\subseteq W$
of codimension $s$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:avoidance}
 W'\cap A=\varnothing,\qquad
 \E_{x\in W'}f(x)\leq
 \frac{\E_{x\in W}f(x)}{1-Mp^{-s}}.
\end{equation}
The same assertion holds after translating the ambient space.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $Q=W/H$, let $\pi:W\to Q$ be the quotient map, and choose a set
$S\subseteq Q$ with $|S|\leq M$ such that $A\subseteq\pi^{-1}(S)$.
Choose uniformly a codimension-$s$ affine subspace $U$ of $Q$.
Every point of $Q$ belongs to $U$ with probability $p^{-s}$.
Therefore the event $\mathcal G=\{U\cap S=\varnothing\}$ satisfies
\[
 \Prob(\mathcal G)\geq1-|S|p^{-s}\geq1-Mp^{-s}>0.
\]
Set $Z(U)=\E_{x\in\pi^{-1}(U)}f(x)$. All pullbacks have the same size,
$|W|p^{-s}$, so uniform point inclusion gives
\[
 \E Z(U)=\E_{x\in W}f(x).
\]
Since $Z\geq0$,
\[
 \E[Z\mid\mathcal G]
 =\frac{\E[Z\ind_{\mathcal G}]}{\Prob(\mathcal G)}
 \leq\frac{\E_{x\in W}f(x)}{1-Mp^{-s}}.
\]
Some successful $U$ has at most this conditional mean. Its pullback is the
required $W'$. In particular, the proof needs no independence between
avoidance and the density of $f$.
\end{proof}

Taking $f$ to be the indicator of the union of all surviving colours, and
choosing $Mp^{-s}\leq1/(2k)$, incurs only the density factor
$(1-1/(2k))^{-1}$. This factor, rather than a fixed factor larger than one,
is what can be iterated $k$ times without an exponentially small initial
coloured density.

\subsection{The density increment and its odds potential}

\begin{lemma}[Hyperplane increment]\label{lem:increment}
Suppose $A,R\subseteq\Fp^m$ have densities $\beta>0$ and $a>0$, and
$(A+A)\cap(2R)=\varnothing$. Then some affine hyperplane has $R$-density
at least $a(1+\beta/2)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use normalized Fourier coefficients. Orthogonality and the disjointness
hypothesis imply
\[
 0=a\beta^2+
 \sum_{\chi\ne1}\widehat{\ind_A}(\chi)^2
                  \overline{\widehat{\ind_{2R}}(\chi)}.
\]
Parseval gives
$\sum_{\chi\ne1}|\widehat{\ind_A}(\chi)|^2=\beta(1-\beta)\leq\beta$.
Consequently some nontrivial character has
$|\widehat{\ind_{2R}}(\chi)|\geq a\beta$.
Let $a_0,\ldots,a_{p-1}$ be the densities of $2R$ in the cosets of
$\ker\chi$. Their mean is $a$. With $u_j=a_j-a$, one has
$\sum_j u_j=0$, hence
\[
 |\widehat{\ind_{2R}}(\chi)|
 \leq\frac1p\sum_j|u_j|
 =\frac2p\sum_{u_j>0}u_j
 \leq2\max_j u_j.
\]
Thus some coset has density at least $a+a\beta/2$.
Applying multiplication by $2^{-1}$ gives an affine hyperplane with the
same density for $R$.
\end{proof}

The $p=3$ increment is the elementary Fourier input of the primary source
\cite{midpoint}; the formulation above covers every odd prime. The
following potential makes the dependence on the starting density explicit.

\begin{lemma}[Odds growth]\label{lem:odds}
In an admissible partition with at most $k$ colours and remainder density
$a\in(0,1)$, there is a hyperplane restriction whose new remainder density
$a'$ either equals one or satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odds}
 \frac{a'}{1-a'}\geq
 \left(1+\frac1{2k}\right)\frac{a}{1-a}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The largest colour has density at least $(1-a)/k$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:increment},
$a'\geq a(1+(1-a)/(2k))$. Since $x/(1-x)$ is increasing on $(0,1)$,
\[
 \frac{a'/(1-a')}{a/(1-a)}
 \geq\frac{1+(1-a)/(2k)}{1-a/(2k)}
 =1+\frac1{2k-a}\geq1+\frac1{2k}.
\]
A full hyperplane terminates the process instead.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Removing all colours at polynomially small density}

\begin{lemma}[High-density clearance]\label{lem:clearance}
With $M,s,\tau$ from \eqref{eq:s}--\eqref{eq:tauT}, let an admissible
partition of an affine space $W$ have total coloured density at most
$\tau$. Then $R\cap W$ contains an affine subspace of relative codimension
at most $ks$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Translate $W$ to a vector space as needed. Suppose the current ambient
space is $W_j$, its coloured union is $B_j$, and its coloured density is
$\delta_j$. If $B_j$ is empty, stop. Otherwise choose a largest nonempty
colour $A$ among the surviving labels. Admissibility gives
\[
 |A+A|\leq |B_j|\leq k|A|.
\]
The first inequality holds because doubling is a bijection on an
odd-characteristic vector space and $A+A$ avoids twice the remainder.
PFR covers $A$ by at most $M$ cosets of a subspace $H$ satisfying
\[
 |H|\leq|A|\leq\delta_j|W_j|.
\]
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:avoidance} with $f=\ind_{B_j}$ and the fixed
codimension $s$. It removes $A$ completely and yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
 \delta_{j+1}\leq\frac{\delta_j}{1-Mp^{-s}}
 \leq\frac{\delta_j}{1-1/(2k)}.
\end{equation}

For completeness, the required quotient dimension is available at every
step; it is not an additional assumption. Inductively, after at most $k$
steps,
\[
 \delta_j\leq\tau\left(1-\frac1{2k}\right)^{-j}\leq2\tau
 =\frac1{2p^s}.
\]
The second inequality follows from Bernoulli's inequality
$(1-1/(2k))^k\geq1/2$. Therefore
$|H|/|W_j|\leq1/(2p^s)<p^{-s}$, so $\dim(W_j/H)>s$.
In particular, any space too small for this restriction must already have
no coloured points. This also makes the induction in \eqref{eq:loss}
noncircular: the bound at step $j$ validates the next application.

Each successful restriction removes at least one colour label. After at
most $k$ restrictions no labels remain, and the total codimension cost is
at most $ks$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Completion of the transfer proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}]
If $R$ is the entire ambient space, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise
iterate Lemma~\ref{lem:odds} until the remainder has density at least
$1-\tau$. Admissibility is inherited by affine restriction because a
midpoint of points in an affine space remains in that space.
The initial odds are at least $\alpha$. Also
\[
 \log\left(1+\frac1{2k}\right)\geq\frac1{2k+1}.
\]
If no earlier restriction is full, after $T$ steps the odds are at least
\[
 \alpha\exp\left(\frac{T}{2k+1}\right)\geq4p^s=\tau^{-1}.
\]
The coloured density is then at most $1/(1+\tau^{-1})<\tau$.
If dimension reaches zero earlier, positive remainder density means the
single point is already in $R$.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:clearance} in the resulting affine space. The total
cost is at most $T+ks$. This proves the explicit bound. Under
\eqref{eq:coverfunction}, $\log M=O(p^3\log(2k))$, and substitution into
\eqref{eq:s}--\eqref{eq:tauT} yields the stated asymptotic bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What changed in the proof mechanism]
A constant density inflation at each of $k$ eliminations would require an
exponentially small coloured density at the start of clearance. The
weighted avoidance lemma instead sets each failure probability to at most
$1/(2k)$, so the total inflation is at most two. PFR then requires only a
polynomially small coloured density. Reaching that threshold takes
$O_p(k\log k)$ hyperplane increments, and clearance also costs
$O_p(k\log k)$. Merely replacing an old theorem by PFR without controlling
these density losses would not establish the improvement.
\end{remark}

\section{A deterministic construction with exact linear loss}

\begin{proposition}[Block-zero construction]\label{prop:block}
Let $p$ be an odd prime, $k,b\geq1$, and $n\geq kb$. There is an admissible
partition of $\Fp^n$ into $k$ colours and a remainder $R$ for which
\[
 \frac{|R|}{p^n}=(1-p^{-b})^k,
 \qquad
 \max\{\dim V:V\subseteq R\text{ affine}\}=n-k.
\]
In particular, taking $b=\lceil\log_p(10k)\rceil$ gives density at least
$9/10$ and exact loss $k$ whenever $n\geq k\lceil\log_p(10k)\rceil$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write a point as $(x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(k)},y)$, with
$x^{(i)}\in\Fp^b$ and $y\in\Fp^{n-kb}$. Put
\[
 R=\{x:x^{(i)}\ne0\text{ for every }i\},
\]
and colour every point outside $R$ by the index of its first zero block.
If two points have colour $i$, their $i$th blocks are zero, as is that of
their midpoint. The midpoint is therefore outside $R$, irrespective of
its other blocks. This proves admissibility. Counting independent blocks
gives the stated density.

Let $V\subseteq R$ be affine. Its image under each block projection is an
affine subspace $a_i+L_i\subseteq\Fp^b$ avoiding zero. Thus $a_i\notin L_i$,
and some linear functional $\ell_i$ vanishes on $L_i$ and satisfies
$\ell_i(a_i)=1$. The pullbacks of these $k$ functionals to the ambient
space are linearly independent, because they are nonzero and have disjoint
coordinate supports. Consequently $V$ satisfies $k$ independent affine
linear equations and $\dim V\leq n-k$.

Conversely, requiring the first coordinate in each block to equal one
leaves an affine subspace of dimension $n-k$ wholly inside $R$.
Finally, Bernoulli's inequality gives
$(1-p^{-b})^k\geq1-kp^{-b}\geq9/10$ for the chosen $b$.
\end{proof}

This is an explicit companion to the lower-construction direction already
noted in the primary source, not a claim to have discovered the necessity
of linear loss for the first time. Together with
Corollary~\ref{cor:q4296}, it leaves a factor $O(\log k)$ between necessary
and sufficient codimension dependence, for the ambient dimensions covered
by the construction. It does not determine the exact sharp function of
$(n,k)$.

\section{Certified entropy-ratio improvement for Q4660}

Let $X'$ be an independent copy of a discrete real-valued random variable
$X$ with finite Shannon entropy. Write
\[
 \Delta_+(X)=H(X+X')-H(X),\qquad
 \Delta_-(X)=H(X-X')-H(X).
\]
Entropy may be measured in nats or bits; the ratio
$\mathcal R(X)=\Delta_-(X)/\Delta_+(X)$ is unchanged. The known universal
inequality is $\Delta_-(X)\leq2\Delta_+(X)$, while sharpness of two is the
question recorded in Q4660 \cite{entropy}. Let $C_*$ denote the least
universal constant.

\begin{proposition}[Two exact finite witnesses]\label{prop:entropy}
For the law on $\{0,1,2,3,4\}$ with probability vector
\[
 \frac1{10000}(8830,1009,143,16,2),
\]
one has
\begin{align*}
 1.405025826013192214095918189356
 &<\mathcal R(X)\\
 &<1.405025826013192214095918189357.
\end{align*}
For the law on $\{0,1,\ldots,7\}$ with denominator $10^8$ and numerators
\begin{align*}
 &(88162304,\ 10154519,\ 1485728,\ 177393,\\[-2pt]
 &\hspace{19mm}18242,\ 1665,\ 138,\ 11),
\end{align*}
one has
\begin{align*}
 1.405180966075633572545128347101
 &<\mathcal R(X)\\
 &<1.405180966075633572545128347102.
\end{align*}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Cbound}
 \boxed{\quad C_*>\frac{70259}{50000}=1.40518.\quad}
\end{equation}
The supplied handoff's lower witness is strictly improved.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Exact certificate and its mathematical justification]
For integer probability weights $w_i$ with total $D$, the output weights
are obtained without rounding:
\[
 s_t=\sum_{i+j=t}w_iw_j,\qquad
 d_t=\sum_{i-j=t}w_iw_j.
\]
Each output has total weight $D^2$. Entropy of any count vector $c$ with
total $N$ is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropycounts}
 H(c)=\log N-\frac1N\sum_{c_i>0}c_i\log c_i.
\end{equation}
All logarithms in the certificate are enclosed by rational bounds.
For $1\leq r\leq2$, put $t=(r-1)/(r+1)\in[0,1/3]$. Then
\begin{align}
 L_J(r)&=2\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\frac{t^{2j+1}}{2j+1},\\
 0\leq\log r-L_J(r)&\leq
 \frac{2t^{2J+1}}{(2J+1)(1-t^2)}.\label{eq:logtail}
\end{align}
The upper bound follows by bounding every denominator in the positive
tail below by $2J+1$ and summing the resulting geometric series.
For a positive integer $m$, write $m=2^e r$ with $1\leq r<2$ and apply
these bounds to $\log m=e\log2+\log r$.

The supplied script \texttt{certify\_entropy.py} uses $J=90$, computes the
series and tail with exact fractions, and rounds each logarithm interval
outward to a grid of mesh $10^{-65}$. Substitution in
\eqref{eq:entropycounts} and outward interval subtraction give positive
lower bounds for both increments. If $L_\pm\leq\Delta_\pm\leq U_\pm$
with $L_+>0$, then
\[
 \frac{L_-}{U_+}\leq\mathcal R(X)\leq\frac{U_-}{L_+}.
\]
The two resulting enclosing intervals lie strictly between the displayed
endpoints. The script also verifies the positive rational lower bound
\[
 L_- -\frac{70259}{50000}U_+>0
\]
for the eight-point law, and the analogous inequality with $281/200$ for
the five-point law. All weights, interval endpoints, and positive-gap
receipts are retained in \texttt{ENTROPY-CERTIFICATE.json}.
The displayed values are therefore exact enclosures, not optimizer output.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The witness survives arbitrarily large input entropy}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:radix}
For every $h>0$ there is a positive-integer-valued $Y$ with $H(Y)>h$ and
$\mathcal R(Y)>1.40518$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $Z$ have the eight-point law, let $Z_0,\ldots,Z_{m-1}$ be independent
copies, and put
\[
 Y=1+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}16^j Z_j.
\]
The digit encodings for the input and the sums are injective: input digits
lie in $[0,7]$ and sum digits in $[0,14]$. Difference digits lie in
$[-7,7]$ and also have a unique base-16 encoding. Indeed, a difference
between two such encodings has digits in $[-14,14]$; a nonzero highest
term cannot be cancelled by all the lower terms because
$14\sum_{j<r}16^j<16^r$.

Thus all relevant vector entropies are preserved by encoding. Coordinate
independence gives
\[
 H(Y)=mH(Z),\qquad
 \Delta_+(Y)=m\Delta_+(Z),\qquad
 \Delta_-(Y)=m\Delta_-(Z).
\]
The ratio is unchanged, while $mH(Z)$ is arbitrarily large.
\end{proof}

This use of tensorization does not amplify a fixed ratio toward two.
It shows that the improved obstruction persists at every entropy scale.
It does not improve the universal upper constant or the separate
sum--product target Q4659.

\section{Verification, limitations, and the next mathematical obstacle}

\subsection{Reproduction}

Run the following from the extracted result directory with Python 3.10 or
later. The certificate scripts use only the standard library.
\begin{verbatim}
python certify_entropy.py
python check_midpoint_lemmas.py
python check_block_construction.py
\end{verbatim}

The midpoint checker examines all $282339$ labelled partitions of
$\Fthree^2$ using one, two, or three colour labels plus the remainder.
Among them it checks the increment and odds inequalities for all $29706$
admissible partitions with nonempty proper remainder. The quotient tests
check all $285184$ allowed cover/test-set pairs in $\Fthree^2$, together
with $6684$ nonnegative-weight controls. A further $9900$ rational odds
checks and $100$ cumulative-loss checks pass. These are exact integer or
fraction comparisons.

The block-construction checker enumerates every affine subspace in each
of eight finite cases. In dimensions three and four, the lists contain
$184$ and $2452$ affine subspaces respectively. Every case attains exactly
$n-k$ as predicted. None of these finite checks is offered as a substitute
for the all-size block proof or Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}.

The entropy certificate itself is a finite exact proof for the displayed
laws. The optional numerical exploration script only explains discovery;
it is not needed for verification, and its local optimizations are not
claims of global optimality.

\subsection{Audit of the critical proof obligations}

The general midpoint proof has four particularly important points.
The small-doubling estimate uses the \emph{total} coloured union in the
current affine space, not the size of the selected colour alone.
The quotient dimension is justified from $|H|\leq|A|$ and the running
density invariant before every avoidance step. Conditional averaging uses
nonnegativity and uniform point inclusion, not an unjustified independence
assumption. Finally, removal of a label is permanent under later nested
restrictions, so there are at most $k$ clearance steps.

The argument is an existence proof. It does not supply a new polynomial-time
algorithm for finding the PFR covers, a numerical value of the absolute
PFR exponent, or a sharp finite-$n$ codimension constant. Small ambient
dimensions are covered by the nonempty-point stopping case.

\subsection{Remaining gaps}

The exact value of the Q4296 extremal function is not determined. The
block construction supplies necessary linear loss, while the new theorem
supplies sufficient $O(k\log k)$ loss. The remaining structural target is
a clearance/density-increment mechanism with an amortized constant
codimension cost per colour, or a construction proving that a
superlinear loss is actually necessary. Improving constants inside the
present PFR input alone does not remove the logarithm, because the
quotient avoidance step still costs logarithmically in the number of
covering cosets.

For Q4660, the certified lower bound is not a universal upper bound and
is not a proof that two is sharp. The local search over short arithmetic
progressions leaves arbitrary supports and arbitrary probability laws
untreated. The correct catalog disposition of both targets is therefore
\emph{partial progress}, with the exact proofs and scopes above retained.
