# Q1961: the five-eighths bound in cyclic 2-groups

12 October 2026. Exact full target: every projective-3-cube-free A⊆Z/(2^n)Z, n≥3, has |A|≤5·2^(n−3). All repetitions and degeneracies count. This remains unresolved here.

Primary [Long–Wagner](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01225), local `texts/papers/collections-6ad9eb9c01fb590d.txt.gz`, Introduction and Section5 reread. Their Theorem1.10 proves the bound for unions of valuation layers. Conjecture5.2 additionally proposes either a pure diagonal cube or a cube generated by (x,3x,y); its second alternative must not be omitted. A recent [primary proof packet](https://prove2.me/theorems/1ebf3f86-4860-4bdd-b603-59334a63f028) states the high-odd-density restricted theorem below; we rederive it independently and make no novelty claim.

<a id="a1"></a>
## A1 — Reduce arbitrary sets to largest layers by ordinary compression

For 2-power cube dimensions Long–Wagner's layered extremizers suggest filling the largest layers first. But at dimension3 this compression changes the forbidden configuration. In Z/32Z the actual extremizer C=L1∪L3 (all16 odd points plus {4,12,20,28}) is cube-free and has20 points. Filling its four even positions instead with {2,6,10,14} produces a set containing the repeated cube {1,2,3}. So the proposed reduction from arbitrary cube-free sets to initial largest layers is false at precisely the conjectured size. The source's appropriate C3 uses nonconsecutive layers; its union-of-layers theorem cannot justify this arbitrary-set operation. Full question survives.

<a id="a2"></a>
## A2 — Induct separately on the parity slices

Write N=2^n,m=N/2,a=|A∩odd|,b=|A∩even|. The even slice, divided by2, is 3-cube-free in Z/mZ, so inductive use of the desired bound gives b≤5m/8. Taking a≤m only gives |A|≤13N/16. The ordinary cyclic2/3 theorem gives a different bound but does not close the five-eighths coefficient.

Cross-parity constraints improve this considerably. If a>3m/4, any four even translates of the odd slice have a common point, by the union bound. Therefore the even slice is sum-free: an even Schur triple u,v,u+v together with a common odd x would give the cube (x,u,v). In particular its subcollection E4 divisible by4 is sum-free inside the subgroup4Z/NZ, and |E4|≤N/8=m/4 (fix a member z and use E4∩(E4+z)=∅).

Let c=m−a. If e≡2 mod4 belongs to A, both odd solutions x of2x=e must violate membership of x or3x, since (x,x,x) would otherwise be a cube. These are two directed tripling edges on the odd residues. Any missing odd vertex meets at most two such directed edges (one outgoing, one incoming, as multiplication by3 is a permutation), so 2|E2|≤2c. Thus |A|≤a+c+m/4=5N/8 in the high-odd branch. This proof also covers n=3; directed edges are counted with multiplicity even when the undirected pair repeats.

Assuming induction for the even slice also handles a≤5m/8. The unresolved range is 5m/8<a≤3m/4. There the two slice estimates allow a+b up to11m/8>5m/4. Thus the proposed parity-induction proof reaches an exact intermediate-density gap, not a full answer. The positive high-odd theorem is retained without resetting the full-target failure count.

<a id="a3"></a>
## A3 — Extend the four-translate intersection lemma to the missing density range

Try to repair A2 by asserting that a>5m/8 already guarantees a common point in O,O−u,O−v,O−u−v for each even Schur triple. This set-theoretic sufficient assertion is false. In Z/32Z let O be all odd residues except {1,3,9,11}; then a=12=3m/4>5m/8. For u=4,v=16 the four translates have empty intersection: each orbit of adding multiples of4 has indices modulo8, the pattern of shifts is {0,1,4,5}, and the missing indices {0,2} hit every translate of that pattern. The even triple {4,16,20} is itself 3-cube-free, so the gap is not a forbidden cube already inside that even triple.

The combined A=O∪{4,16,20} does contain another mixed cube, (4,21,27). This witness therefore refutes only the universal **four-intersection sufficient lemma**, not Q1961 or a use of all mixed cubes. A full proof needs to exploit those additional configurations instead of lowering the union-bound threshold. This is distinct from A2's numerical induction gap: it supplies a concrete counterexample to its proposed repair.

<a id="a4"></a>
## A4 — Average under units and use all linear cube constraints

Multiplication by odd units preserves cube freeness and averages vertex marginals to valuation-layer variables. The tempting next step is to optimize just 0≤a_y≤1 and Σ_{y∈C}a_y≤|C|−1 for every actual cube. This relaxation is too weak even with complete layer symmetry. Set a_0=0 and a_y=2/3 for all nonzero y. A cube containing0 has its inequality automatically. A cube omitting0 has at least three distinct points. To prove this directly, if it had at most two, two of its three generators would equal x. Then x,2x are distinct and nonzero, and the third generator is x or2x. In either case the cube contains3x, which cannot equal x (that would force2x=0) or2x (that would force x=0). Therefore (2/3)|C|≤|C|−1.

The total is2(N−1)/3>5N/8 whenever N>16, and the assignment is already invariant under every unit. It is an exact all-size fractional obstruction to this proposed symmetrization-plus-linear-relaxation method. An averaged random cube-free set has additional joint constraints; they cannot be replaced by these marginal inequalities. This is distinct from A1's deterministic compression failure.

<a id="a5"></a>
## A5 — Keep only the pure-diagonal alternative in the source's stronger route

In Z/32Z take D=L1∪L3∪{16}, of size21>20. It avoids every {x,2x,3x}: for odd x the double lies in the excluded layer L2; an L2 element is itself excluded; for x∈L3 its double is8 or24, excluded; and x=16 has double0, also excluded. All other x are excluded or have a0 multiple. This includes x=0.

Pulling back D modulo32 gives diagonal avoidance and density21/32>5/8 in every Z/(2^n)Z with n≥5, because0 is excluded in the base. But D contains the mixed cube (1,4,12), with sums {1,4,5,12,13,16,17}. Thus pure diagonal avoidance cannot prove the bound. Long–Wagner's stronger Conjecture5.2 retains (x,3x,y); indeed this witness is exactly of that form, with x=4,y=1. The second alternative remains an unproved full sufficient route; this attempt does not assert that it fails. The multiplier-only weakening is distinct from an order compression or an LP marginal relaxation.

## Disposition

Five worked full-target routes failed, with one useful restricted high-odd theorem. None resets the full-target counter or refutes the conjecture. Request independent count/scope review; exact controls are in `check_cubes.py` and `CUBE-CHECK-RESULTS.json`.
