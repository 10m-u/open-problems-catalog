# Independent review of the unique-optimum 1-tree obstruction

I read [the main proof](../generating-graph/RESULT.md) in full. The argument is sound under its stated scope: for each $k\geq17$, it constructs a strictly metric integer-cost instance with a unique optimum but no possible strict forced-rooted-1-tree deletion at any real vertex potentials. This review does not assert originality or external peer review.

The local fractional cut argument correctly handles all proper subsets of a Petersen gadget. With one crossing spoke, at least one cycle side is partial; otherwise both cycle sides are empty/full and the number of crossing spokes is zero or five. A partial cycle contributes at least one through its two half-weight cut edges. Deleting the designated spoke loses exactly one when the ports are separated. Globally, two partial gadgets suffice; one partial gadget with separated ports needs and receives a crossing connector by parity on the virtual ring. Proper unions of whole gadgets cross at least two connectors.

The tour lower bound also covers the possibility of using neither connector. If a gadget has no incident expensive tour edge, its only external tour edges are its two cheap connectors. A single global Hamiltonian cycle must cross this proper gadget cut at least twice, so both connectors are used. Removing them leaves one spanning internal path, since a separate internal cycle would be a disconnected component of the global tour. Adding the deleted spoke produces a forbidden Petersen Hamiltonian cycle. Thus every gadget needs an incident expensive edge and at least $\lceil k/2\rceil$ expensive edges are required.

The relabeled forced witness is valid even when its source and target edges overlap. One can first move the first source endpoint to the first target endpoint, then move the current image of the second source endpoint to the second target endpoint; the second swap need not move the first target. At most four vertices move. The original fractional mass on their incident edges is at most their degree sum, eight, so the primary cost increases by at most eight. Relabeling preserves the complete-graph subtour constraints and fixes the requested coordinate to one.

The arbitrary-potential domination is correct. Removing a root leaves a fractional spanning-tree vector, with the exact total mass and subset inequalities stated in the main proof. For any sorted signed weights, every prefix has fractional mass at most its graphic rank; Kruskal attains that rank. The summation-by-parts formula therefore bounds the fractional cost below by the minimum tree cost, regardless of the weight signs. Contracting a forced nonroot edge with unit coordinate preserves the subset inequalities after subtracting that coordinate. Root-spoke forcing instead reduces to selecting one cheapest distinct remaining spoke. Fractional degree two cancels every vertex potential exactly.

Finally, $d_e=B(a_e+1)+2^{\operatorname{rank}(e)}$ has maximum single-edge cost at most $7B/2$, and every two-edge path costs more than $4B$, proving strict triangle inequalities. Binary subset sums are unique and below $B$, so the optimum tour is unique and the primary order is preserved. The forced fractional witness is strictly below $B(2n+9)$; $k\geq17$ makes $\lceil k/2\rceil\geq9$. This supplies the strict separation below the integer optimum even at $k=17$.

The conclusion concerns this lower-bound family. It rules out a universal claim that uniqueness alone makes these certificates prune to a sparse or bounded-width graph. It leaves stronger cuts, branching, combinatorial arguments, and selected-optimum methods available. The exponential numerical scale has polynomial bit length; no constant-magnitude cost claim is needed.
