# Independent review of the path- and cycle-power routes

Reviewed internally on 12 October 2026. The five specified sufficient routes for **each** of Q1852 and Q1853 fail as stated, with the strengthened Q1853-A1 example below. Every example is Hamiltonian. These are rejected reductions and construction rules, not counterexamples to either conjecture and not proofs that all possible methods fail. No novelty or external peer-review claim is made.

I read [POWER-ATTEMPTS.md](../other-targets/POWER-ATTEMPTS.md), [check_power_routes.py](../other-targets/check_power_routes.py), its receipt, the exact catalog statements, and the local publisher text of [Espuny Díaz et al., *Dirac's theorem for graphs of bounded bandwidth*](https://doi.org/10.37236/13474). The local source is collections-295e0a79f41bcbaa.txt.gz. The source's Theorem 5 with $\ell=2$ requires $\deg(v_i)\ge\min(i+1,k+2,k+n-i)$ in one-based order. Theorem 2 requires effective degree $k+2$, namely the minimum of that number and the host degree. Q1852's half-host threshold and Q1853's $k+1$ threshold differ from these sufficient conditions.

## Q1852

1. **Asymmetric theorem transfer.** In $P_{20}^6$ after the two indicated deletions, vertices 0 and 19 both have degree 5, while their host degree is 6 and their Q1852 threshold is 5. Whichever natural orientation is chosen, Theorem 5 asks for degree 6 at its final endpoint. The effective-degree condition fails too. Relabeling does not rescue Theorem 2 at width 6, because every host position has degree at least 6. Choosing a smaller width cannot fit the degree-12 vertices. This rejects the direct transfer, while leaving a symmetric boundary theorem open.

2. **Global closure.** For $P_{25}^4$, every host degree lies between 4 and 8, so the conjectural degree promise holds, but every nonedge degree sum is at most $16<25$. Bondy–Chvátal closure has no first addition and therefore no later additions. Dirac's minimum-degree condition also fails. For $n>4k$ and $k\ge4$, the same conclusion follows from maximum degree $2k$ and minimum degree $k$. This rejects these certificates specifically, not every result describable as a degree-sequence theorem.

3. **Stronger separators.** The two overlapping $P_7^4$ copies satisfy all half-host thresholds in $P_{12}^4$. Removing their two shared vertices leaves two $K_5$ components. Thus vertex connectivity is at most 2, and toughness is at most $2/2=1$, excluding both 4-connectivity and 2-toughness. The proposed zigzag Hamilton cycle exists. This is precisely compatible with the source's Example 4 at $k=4,\ell=3,n=12$: the source construction blocks three internally disjoint spanning branches, rather than ordinary Hamiltonicity. Both stronger separator properties belong to this single route, not two separate failures.

4. **Promised subtour integrality.** $P_{10}^9=K_{10}$ satisfies the target promise. The unit-spoke/half-cycle Petersen point has degree 2, all proper cuts at least 2, and a genuinely fractional extreme point. For completeness, even if upper bounds $x_e\le1$ are not explicitly listed, they follow from the degree equations and the two-vertex cut inequalities. Fix the 30 unsupported edges at zero and the five spokes at one. The remaining degree equations on each odd five-cycle uniquely force all ten supported cycle variables to $1/2$. Hence these tight constraints define a singleton face. The independent rational rank calculation below confirms rank 15 on the support, so the claimed vertex property is verified rather than assumed.

5. **Endpoint deletion induction.** Vertex 4 initially has degree 6; deleting endpoint 0 loses its incident edge, giving degree 5 against new host degree 7 and required integer degree 6. The mirrored obstruction occurs at vertex 7 on deleting endpoint 11. The zigzag tour avoids all three deleted edges. Only inheritance of this exact promise fails; repaired induction classes remain possible.

## Q1853

1. **Path-theorem transfer after a cyclic cut: scope repair needed and supplied.** The initial $C_{20}^2\subset C_{20}^3$ example blocks transfer at the same $k=3$, but is insufficient to reject a transfer that optimizes the width: after a cut it is $P_{20}^2$, to which the known path theorem applies at width 2. The main thread agreed to strengthen the example to

   \[
   H=C_{20}^2+\{\{0,3\},\{10,13\}\}\subset C_{20}^3.
   \]

   Its minimum degree is 4. Every cyclic cut, retaining edges with linear distance at most 3, leaves a vertex of degree 5 and at least 16 vertices of degree at most 4. Width 2 is impossible because a graph of bandwidth 2 has maximum degree at most 4. For width $k'\ge3$, Theorem 5 permits degree at most 4 in at most five positions; for $k'\ge6$, its final endpoint already demands at least 6, beyond the graph's maximum degree. Theorem 2's effective requirements also cannot accommodate the low-degree vertices. Thus no relabeling of any cut can meet either theorem at any width. This stronger obstruction is valid and preserves the natural undirected Hamilton cycle.

2. **Global closure.** The full $C_{25}^4$ has degree 8, satisfies the promise $\delta\ge5$, and has no nonedge degree sum reaching 25. For $n>4k$, the same lack of a first closure step holds. Dirac also fails. This is one route, even though two closely related degree certificates are named.

3. **Stronger connectivity.** The two $P_5^3$ copies sharing vertex 4, plus edge $\{0,8\}$, lie in $C_9^3$ and have minimum degree 4. Deleting vertices 0 and 4 leaves components of sizes 3 and 4. Hence 3-connectivity does not follow. The natural cycle is present; Hamiltonicity needs no such stronger connectivity implication.

4. **Promised subtour integrality.** $C_{10}^5=K_{10}$ has minimum degree 9 and satisfies the target $k+1=6$. The same fractional extreme point from Q1852-A4 is valid here. Reusing this witness is legitimate because the two catalog conjectures are separate and their promises are each checked. It is not a second different LP obstruction within either individual problem.

5. **Only-forward construction.** For odd $n\ge7$, the graph $C_n^2-\{\{0,1\},\{2,4\}\}$ has minimum degree 3. Every natural forward arc advances 1 or 2 modulo $n$. A directed Hamilton cycle has $n$ arcs and total advance in $[n,2n]$, divisible by $n$, so every advance must be 1 or every advance must be 2. Each possibility is blocked by a deleted edge. The supplied undirected tour $0,2,1,3,4,\ldots,n-1,0$ avoids both deletions. Thus restricting to this orientation loses valid cycles. The sum argument covers all asserted sizes; the four finite sizes are controls only.

## Independent evidence and counting limits

[check_power_review.py](check_power_review.py) does not import the main checker. Its exact controls verify all 20 cuts of the strengthened cycle example and **680** impossibilities of assigning the resulting degrees to the two theorems' requirements for widths 3 through 19, even ignoring the bandwidth restrictions. They check the Petersen support's defining constraints have rational rank 15, all **1,022** proper cuts satisfy the relaxation, and its cheap support has no Hamilton cycle. Receipt: [POWER-REVIEW-CHECK-RESULTS.json](POWER-REVIEW-CHECK-RESULTS.json). The main checker covered all other stated graph containment, degree, separator and tour claims; its mathematical formulas were independently rederived in this review.

The fractional point does not prevent an algorithm from deliberately finding an integral feasible point. It refutes the assertion that this full relaxation becomes integral under either promise, or that arbitrary objective optimization must return a Hamilton cycle. To see the practical LP gap, assign cost 1 to the Petersen support and 2 elsewhere in $K_{10}$. The fractional optimum is 10, since its total mass is 10 and every edge costs at least 1. Every Hamilton cycle has cost at least 11 because the cheap support has no Hamilton cycle. This is also an objective-level obstruction, while $K_{10}$ itself is plainly Hamiltonian.

Within each problem the five classes are distinct: existing-theorem transfer, global degree closure, stronger separator implication, relaxation integrality, and a particular recursive/oriented construction. Mirroring an order, citing both Dirac and closure, citing both connectivity and toughness, or checking additional sizes does not increase the count. The cross-problem reuse of the closure and LP examples is explicitly scoped to each separate promise. The record supports **five rejected specified sufficient routes per problem**, not a mathematical assertion that either full conjecture is false or beyond reach. Any use of the user's stopping rule should retain that distinction and the unresolved catalog status.
