# Independent review of the five Q1953 routes

**Verdict:** [Q1953-ATTEMPTS.md](../other-targets/Q1953-ATTEMPTS.md) contains five valid failures of specified sufficient construction routes. The conjecture remains unresolved. The examples are Hamiltonian; none is a bicirculant counterexample. I accept the per-problem count while keeping the shared arithmetic mechanism and precise restrictions below.

I read the packet, both Haar checkers and receipts, and the relevant retained primary text, also available as [Bonvicini–Pisanski–Žitnik](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21607). The source's Theorem 1.3 reduces the remaining |S|≥4 part to connected cyclic Haar graphs. Theorem 2.1 concerns connected **cubic cyclic Haar** graphs; Lemma 2.3 guarantees a Hamilton path. The stated Theorem 1.5(iii),(v) special cases apply to A1 and A2 respectively.

| Attempt | Exact rejected claim | What remains possible |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Collective gcd one supplies a pair of offsets with unit difference | A cycle using more than two offsets |
| A2 | Every connected Haar graph has a connected three-offset spanning union | A non-invariant cubic spanning subgraph, or a higher-valence construction |
| A3 | Every disconnected two-offset cover admits a two-edge merge | Larger alternating exchanges or changing the initial cover |
| A4 | Any selected quotient cycle, with the selected lifts, is spanning upstairs | Choosing representatives and controlling voltage |
| A5 | Applying an automorphism to a Hamilton path can close its nonadjacent ends | A suitable path construction or path rotations that change its edges |

The alternating-walk subgroup is generated by S−S, so connectivity is exactly gcd(m,S−S)=1. A pair r,s yields gcd(m,s−r) cycles. For the three-prime A1 family, every pair difference shares a prime but all differences generate Z_m. The earlier [FINAL-ROUTE-REVIEW.md](FINAL-ROUTE-REVIEW.md) gives an additional safeguard: its three nonzero offsets already form a connected cubic Haar subgraph, another known Hamiltonicity proof for that family.

For A2, any three offsets omit some nonzero m/p. Every selected offset is divisible by that omitted p, so no selected cubic union is connected. This genuinely blocks the stronger extraction proposal, although it is the same Chinese-remainder obstruction as A1 with a changed arity and a different source theorem.

For A3, at m=24t all twelve nonzero ordered differences of {0,1,4,10} are distinct modulo m. Two outer vertices therefore have at most one common neighbor, excluding every four-cycle. A nontrivial degree-preserving replacement of two edges by two edges in this simple bipartite graph would use the four edges of a four-cycle. Consequently the four-component {0,4} cover admits no such move, even though {0,1} is a Hamilton cycle.

For A4, modulo q the offsets 0 and q coincide. Keep the offset labels (or parallel quotient edges) when discussing the lift: this is not a unique simple-edge representative. The particular representatives 0,p give p cycles of length 2q upstairs, despite a Hamilton cycle on the q-index quotient and connectedness provided by the unused q offset. The packet explicitly restricts its conclusion to that chosen lift; it does not show that every representative choice fails.

For A5, the recorded path 0,5,3,8,2,7,1,6,4,9 in H(5,{0,1,2}) uses every vertex and valid edges, but its endpoints are nonadjacent. Every graph automorphism preserves this fact. The checker's simultaneous-shift controls are examples; the all-automorphism assertion follows from adjacency preservation and needs no automorphism enumeration. This does not obstruct Pósa rotations or choosing another path.

**Counting:** A1/A2 share the subgroup obstruction, and A4 uses the same pair-cycle formula. Their failed hypotheses concern two-offset direct selection, cubic-theorem extraction, and quotient-cycle lifting respectively. A3 tests local exchanges and A5 tests whole-path symmetry. They are five specified routes, not five algebraically independent obstruction theorems. The known Hamiltonicity of these instances must not be recorded as a new catalog resolution.
