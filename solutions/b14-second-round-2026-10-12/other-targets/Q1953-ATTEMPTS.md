# Q1953 — Attempts on bicirculant Hamiltonicity

The exact target concerns every connected regular bicirculant, with the catalog's two exceptions. The primary paper reduces the outstanding part to cyclic Haar graphs; known special cases must not be recast as new solutions. The stopping rule is five consecutive unsuccessful attempts **on Q1953**, independently of other B14 questions.

## Q1953-A1: select two offset matchings

**Proposed route.** In a cyclic Haar graph H(m,S), the two vertex classes are u_i and v_i, with edges u_i v_(i+s) for s in S, indices modulo m. Every offset is a perfect matching. If some r,s have gcd(m,s-r)=1, their union is a Hamilton cycle. Try to show connectedness always supplies such a pair.

**What was checked.** Alternating the two matchings changes an index by s-r; their union consists of gcd(m,s-r) cycles, each of length 2m/gcd(m,s-r). Connectedness of the full graph is equivalent to gcd(m,S-S)=1. The equivalence follows because even walks realize the subgroup generated by offset differences; odd walks switch the vertex class.

**Obstruction.** Let m=pqr for three distinct odd primes, and S={0,pq,pr,qr}. The three nonzero differences from zero have collective gcd one with m, so H is connected and 4-regular. Every difference between a pair of nonzero offsets shares a prime with m: pq-pr shares p, pq-qr shares q, and pr-qr shares r. Differences from zero are nonunits too. Every two-offset union is disconnected.

For m=105, S={0,15,21,35}; the six gcds are 15,21,35,3,5,7. The exact checker confirms full connectedness and each cycle decomposition on four instances. This is an all-size family obstructing the proposed selection lemma.

**Outcome:** unsuccessful full-target attempt, streak 1. This does not refute Q1953. Indeed the source's Theorem 1.5(iii) covers odd m with at most three prime-power factors, including this family. Hamilton cycles here must use more than two offsets. No claim of novelty is made.

**Evidence:** [exact checker](check_initial_routes.py), [receipt](INITIAL-ROUTE-CHECK-RESULTS.json), [independent review](../initialization/FINAL-ROUTE-REVIEW.md). [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21607), local text at ../../../texts/papers/collections-f0c6c0c22c39f930.txt.gz.

## Q1953-A2: extract a connected cubic offset subgraph

**Proposed route.** The source's Theorem 2.1 settles every connected cubic cyclic Haar graph. Try to extract three offsets with a connected spanning union from every connected higher-valence Haar graph.

**Obstruction.** Let m be the product of four distinct odd primes and S={0}∪{m/p:p divides m}. The full Haar graph is connected: the offset differences collectively generate all residues. Any three selected offsets omit at least one nonzero offset m/p. Every chosen offset is divisible by that omitted prime p, so their differences share it and the spanning subgraph is disconnected. No three-offset selection gives a connected cubic graph. This is different from selecting two offsets directly; even the general cubic Hamiltonicity theorem cannot apply via this extraction.

The checker covers three such squarefree m. These full graphs are Hamiltonian by the source's Theorem 1.5(v); this is a failure of extraction, not of the conjecture. **Outcome:** unsuccessful; streak 2. Arbitrary connected cubic spanning subgraphs need not consist of whole offset orbits, and have not been ruled out.

## Q1953-A3: merge a cycle cover with two-edge switches

**Proposed route.** Start with two offset matchings, then repeatedly merge components by deleting two cover edges and adding two graph edges. Try to infer an available switch whenever the cover is disconnected.

**Obstruction.** For m=24t, t≥1, take S={0,1,4,10}. All six positive differences are 1,3,4,6,9,10; they are distinct, and their negatives modulo m are distinct from them. Thus two outer vertices cannot have two common inner neighbors: a four-cycle would require two equal ordered offset differences. The graph has no four-cycle.

The offsets {0,4} give four cycle-cover components. Every nontrivial two-edge switch preserving degrees would form a four-cycle from its two old and two new edges; none exists. So the cover cannot make even one such move, despite the graph containing the Hamilton cycle from offsets {0,1}. A larger alternating exchange or a different initial cover is necessary. **Outcome:** unsuccessful; streak 3.

## Q1953-A4: lift any quotient Hamilton cycle

**Proposed route.** Quotient by a prime factor, find a Hamilton cycle there, and lift it; connectedness of the full cover is supposed to make the lift spanning.

**Obstruction.** Let m=pq for distinct odd primes and S={0,p,q}. The full Haar graph is connected. Modulo q, offsets {0,p} produce a Hamilton cycle because gcd(p,q)=1. Lifting that chosen cycle with those representatives gives p disjoint cycles of length 2q, because gcd(m,p)=p. Connectedness of the full cover, supplied by the unused offset q, does not repair this chosen lift. An induction must control the cycle's voltage or choose representatives with that control, rather than lift an arbitrary cycle.

The full graph is cubic and Hamiltonian by Theorem 2.1. Four exact examples verify the quotient and lift decompositions. **Outcome:** unsuccessful; streak 4.

## Q1953-A5: close a known Hamilton path by applying an automorphism

**Proposed route.** Lemma 2.3 gives a Hamilton path in every connected cyclic Haar graph. Try to close its endpoints after applying graph automorphisms, exploiting the two vertex orbits.

**Obstruction.** In H(5,{0,1,2}) the checker produces a Hamilton path with nonadjacent endpoints. Every graph automorphism preserves nonadjacency, so applying it to the whole path cannot make those endpoints adjacent. Rotating just one endpoint is not an operation on the path and offers no valid path-completeness argument. The graph itself has a Hamilton cycle from offsets {0,1}; selecting or modifying a suitable Hamilton path remains the missing construction. **Outcome:** unsuccessful; streak 5.

These are five attempted sufficient routes on Q1953, with four different construction stages after the initial two-offset test. Their failures are local proof-route results, not proof that Q1953 is false. Subject to independent review, Q1953 reaches its per-problem stop limit. Other B14 problems continue.
