## Statement and estimator convention

Pásztor, Kassraie and Krause, *Bandits with Preference Feedback: A Stackelberg
Game Perspective*, [arXiv:2406.16745v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16745v3),
§5.2, ask whether both optimization domains in (7) can be enlarged to the whole
action space without losing the regret rate. The answer is yes, with constants
depending on the fixed RKHS bound and regularization parameters.

Use the source's confidence coefficients, dueling kernel, and estimator. The
theoretical estimator is bounded in RKHS norm by $B$: the main text assumes
this bound, and Appendix A supplies the projection when it is not automatic.
The result below retains that convention. It does not establish a guarantee for
the unprojected experimental estimator when its norm exceeds $B$, nor does it
assert efficient exact solution of the continuous max-min problem.

Write $s(a)=(1+e^{-a})^{-1}$. At round $t$, before observing that round's
feedback, let

$$
 h_t(x,y)=g_t(x)-g_t(y),\qquad
 d_t(x,y)=\sigma_t^{\mathrm D}(x,y),\qquad
 \ell_t(x,y)=s(g_t(x)-g_t(y))-\beta_t^{\mathrm D}d_t(x,y).
$$

The history in these quantities contains rounds $1,\ldots,t-1$. This just
makes the source's update indexing explicit. Select any exact optimizers

$$
 x_t\in\mathop{\rm argmax}_{x\in\mathcal X}\inf_{z\in\mathcal X}\ell_t(x,z),
 \qquad
 y_t\in\mathop{\rm argmin}_{y\in\mathcal X}\ell_t(x_t,y).
$$

Extrema are understood to exist, as in the source's exact action-selection
rule. All choices among ties are permitted. Under the usual continuous-kernel
compact-domain setting, continuity gives attainment. The argument also applies
on any domain on which the stated optimizers exist.

Let $x^*$ maximize the true utility $f$, and let

$$
 r_t=\frac{s(f(x^*)-f(x_t))+s(f(x^*)-f(y_t))-1}{2}.
$$

Set

$$
 m_B=\min_{|a|\le 2B}s'(a)=\frac{1}{2+2\cosh(2B)},
 \qquad C_B=1+\frac{1}{4m_B}\ge2,
 \qquad a_0=\lambda\kappa>0.
$$

Here $a_0$ is exactly the variance regularizer already used by the source;
no confidence coefficient is enlarged. The explicit theorem is

$$
 \Pr\!\left\{\forall T\ge1:
 R^{\mathrm D}(T)\le
 C_B\sqrt{\frac{8}{\log(1+4/a_0)}}\,
 \beta_T^{\mathrm D}(\delta)\sqrt{T\gamma_T^{\mathrm D}}
 \right\}\ge1-\delta. \tag{1}
$$

In particular, this is the requested

$$
 R^{\mathrm D}(T)=O_{B,\lambda,\kappa}
 (\beta_T^{\mathrm D}(\delta)\sqrt{T\gamma_T^{\mathrm D}}).
$$

The rate statement does not claim sublinear regret for every possible kernel:
whether the displayed bound is sublinear depends on its information gain.

## 1. The posterior standard deviation is a pseudometric

Take a feature map $\phi:\mathcal X\to\mathcal H$ for $k$. The dueling
feature is $\psi(x,y)=\phi(x)-\phi(y)$. Let

$$
 V_t=a_0 I+\sum_{j<t}\psi(x_j,y_j)\otimes\psi(x_j,y_j).
$$

The finite-rank inverse identity applied to the source's posterior covariance
formula gives

$$
 d_t(x,y)^2=a_0\langle\psi(x,y),V_t^{-1}\psi(x,y)\rangle.
 \tag{2}
$$

Thus $d_t(x,y)=\|\sqrt{a_0}V_t^{-1/2}(\phi(x)-\phi(y))\|$. It is
symmetric, vanishes on the diagonal, and satisfies

$$
 d_t(y,z)\le d_t(y,x)+d_t(x,z). \tag{3}
$$

This remains valid for infinite-dimensional RKHSs: the history term is bounded
and finite rank, and $V_t\succeq a_0I$. No invertibility of a Gram matrix or
strict positive definiteness of $k$ is required.

Also, $k(x,x)\le1$ implies $d_t(x,y)^2\le\|\phi(x)-\phi(y)\|^2\le4$.
Every element of the dueling RKHS is a difference of a scalar function; this
follows directly by applying its feature representation to $\psi(x,y)$.
In particular, the projected estimator still has this difference structure.
The source's norm bound gives

$$
 |g_t(x)-g_t(y)|\le2B. \tag{4}
$$

## 2. A deterministic max-min lemma

Fix one round and suppress $t$. Write $\beta=\beta_t^{\mathrm D}$,
$x=x_t$, $y=y_t$, and

$$
 u=\beta d(x,y),\quad D=g(y)-g(x),\quad
 V(z)=\inf_w\ell(z,w),\quad v=V(x)=\ell(x,y).
$$

**Lemma.** Under (3) and (4),

$$
 D\le u/m_B,\qquad 0\le\tfrac12-v\le C_Bu. \tag{5}
$$

**Proof.** If $D\le0$, the first bound is immediate. If $D>0$, then for
every $z$, both $g(x)-g(z)$ and $g(y)-g(z)$ lie in $[-2B,2B]$.
The mean-value theorem and (3) give

$$
\begin{aligned}
 \ell(y,z)-\ell(x,z)
 &=s(g(y)-g(z))-s(g(x)-g(z))
   -\beta(d(y,z)-d(x,z))\\
 &\ge m_B D-u.
\end{aligned}
$$

Taking infima gives $V(y)\ge V(x)+m_BD-u$. Since $x$ maximizes $V$,
$V(y)\le V(x)$, proving $D\le u/m_B$.

The follower may choose $x$, so $v\le\ell(x,x)=1/2$. Since

$$
 w:=\tfrac12-v=s(D)-\tfrac12+u,
$$

the global bound $s'\le1/4$ implies

$$
 0\le w\le u+\tfrac14\max(D,0)
 \le(1+1/(4m_B))u=C_Bu.
$$

This proves (5). Notice that the lemma uses the leader's optimality. The
follower's comparison with the leader alone would give only $v\le1/2$. ∎

## 3. Both queried actions have regret controlled by their own uncertainty

Work on the source's single uniform confidence event

$$
 |s(g_t(a)-g_t(b))-s(f(a)-f(b))|
 \le\beta_t^{\mathrm D}d_t(a,b)
 \quad\text{for every }t,a,b. \tag{6}
$$

Corollary 5, with its bounded/projected estimator convention, gives this event
probability at least $1-\delta$ for adaptive queries. Set

$$
 a=s(f(x^*)-f(x))-\tfrac12,\qquad
 b=s(f(x^*)-f(y))-\tfrac12.
$$

Both are nonnegative. Since $x^*$ belongs to the unrestricted follower domain,
(6) and the choice of $y$ imply

$$
 a=\tfrac12-s(f(x)-f(x^*))
 \le\tfrac12-\ell(x,x^*)\le\tfrac12-v=w. \tag{7}
$$

If $f(y)\ge f(x)$, then $b\le a\le w$. Otherwise put
$A=f(x^*)-f(x)\ge0$ and $E=f(x)-f(y)>0$. The function

$$
 q(z)=s(z)-\tfrac12=\tfrac12\tanh(z/2)
$$

is subadditive on the nonnegative half-line: the addition formula for
$\tanh$ gives $q(A+E)\le q(A)+q(E)$. Hence

$$
 b\le a+s(f(x)-f(y))-\tfrac12.
$$

Using the upper half of (6) for the selected pair, and
$v=s(g(x)-g(y))-u$, gives

$$
 s(f(x)-f(y))-\tfrac12
 \le s(g(x)-g(y))+u-\tfrac12=2u-w.
$$

Consequently $b\le w+(2u-w)=2u$. Combining the two cases,

$$
 a\le w\le C_Bu,\qquad b\le\max(w,2u)\le C_Bu,
$$

and therefore

$$
 r_t=(a+b)/2\le C_B\beta_t^{\mathrm D}d_t(x_t,y_t). \tag{8}
$$

This handles zero posterior distance as well: then (5) forces $w=0$, and
both regrets vanish. No division by $d_t(x_t,y_t)$ was used.

## 4. Summation and the anytime guarantee

Write $z_t=(x_t,y_t)$ and let $K_T^{\mathrm D}$ be the Gram matrix of the
played dueling features. Repeated Schur complements give

$$
 \sum_{t=1}^T\log(1+d_t(z_t)^2/a_0)
 =\log\det(I+a_0^{-1}K_T^{\mathrm D})\le2\gamma_T^{\mathrm D}.
$$

Concavity of $z\mapsto\log(1+z/a_0)$ on $[0,4]$ implies

$$
 \sum_{t=1}^Td_t(z_t)^2
 \le\frac{4}{\log(1+4/a_0)}
     \sum_{t=1}^T\log(1+d_t(z_t)^2/a_0)
 \le\frac{8\gamma_T^{\mathrm D}}{\log(1+4/a_0)}. \tag{9}
$$

The original exploration coefficient is nondecreasing. Summing (8), using
Cauchy-Schwarz and then (9), proves (1) on event (6). Since the same event
already holds for every round and every query pair, (1) holds simultaneously
for every horizon; there is no further union bound. ∎

## Verification scope

`python check_q726.py` checks finite max-min problems using exact fractions.
Scores are logarithms of rational numbers, so all logistic probabilities and
the slope-based bound are rational; numerical logarithms are unnecessary.
It tests every optimizing leader and every minimizing follower, including ties,
over exhaustive small score grids and independently chosen true utilities.
It also verifies the posterior feature/kernel identity and sequential
determinant factorization over rational matrices. A deliberately invalid
nonmetric uncertainty table is required to fail the regret bound, providing a
negative control for the proof's essential hypothesis.

These computations check the algebra and finite controls. The written argument,
rather than finite enumeration, establishes the infinite-domain and anytime
claims. The source's confidence theorem is a cited dependency, not a theorem
re-proved by this packet.
