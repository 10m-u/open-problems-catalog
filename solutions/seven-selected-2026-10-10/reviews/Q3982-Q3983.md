The reviewer independently opened
[Aurzada–Dutta–Wiegand, arXiv:2610.12354v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12354v1).
The hypotheses are a fixed starting point, a deterministic budget proportional
to the horizon, and a drift that is constant along each trajectory but may
depend on the horizon. The two questions occur in §1.1. The proposed theorem
uses precisely this setup and explicitly separates the deterministic threshold
from the source's exponential-threshold theorem.

For squared drift divided by the horizon tending to zero, the proposed limit
has the parameter requested by Q3982. At nonzero critical drift, the cubic in
the proof gives a different positive parameter, answering Q3983. The theorem
also allows oscillating drift signs. It does not claim a uniform result for
initial points tending to infinity or for observation windows approaching the
conditioning endpoint.

## Independent reconstruction of the measure change

The original drift density through time $T$ is

$$
 \exp\{\mu_T(W_T-x)-\mu_T^2T/2\}.
$$

Upon conditioning, its deterministic factor cancels. Applying the Markov
property under *driftless* Brownian motion yields the ratio of endpoint-tilted
functionals in equation (4). In particular there is no remaining factor
$e^{\mu_TW_t}$ in that ratio. An equivalent calculation that first applies
the Markov property under drifted Brownian motion gives the same cancellation.
The future drift parameter remains $\mu_T$, as the proof states; replacing
it by $\mu_{T-t}$ would be incorrect.

The endpoint-tilted transform was re-derived from its quadratic-exponential
ansatz. The coefficients satisfy

$$
 q'=2s-q^2,\qquad \ell'=-q\ell,\qquad
 c'=(\ell^2-q)/2.
$$

These yield the displayed Mehler transform with the signs and factors of two
in equation (6). Analytic continuation on the right half-plane is legitimate:
the exponential endpoint tilt is integrable, the quadratic killing has
nonnegative real part, and the specified hyperbolic-cosine branch has no zeros
there. The no-atom argument via the independent endpoint and Brownian bridge
also checks out.

## Saddle and error estimates

Differentiating the phase independently gives

$$
 h'(s)=\theta-\frac1{2\sqrt{2s}}-
                 \frac{r}{2(\sqrt{2s})^3},
 \qquad
 h''(s)=\frac1{2(\sqrt{2s})^3}+
                 \frac{3r}{2(\sqrt{2s})^5}.
$$

Thus the real saddle satisfies the stated cubic and has strictly positive
second derivative. As $r$ varies in a bounded nonnegative interval, the
saddle and its curvature remain in compact subsets of the positive half-line.
This is the uniformity needed to cover all subcritical sequences, including
ones that approach critical order extremely slowly.

On the vertical line through the saddle, writing $\sqrt{2s}=\alpha+i\beta$
gives $\alpha^2-\beta^2=\lambda^2$. This proves both
$\alpha\ge\lambda$ and
$\Re(1/\sqrt{2s})\le1/\lambda$. The squared-drift contribution therefore
helps the real-part bound rather than worsening it. Equation (11) genuinely
controls the entire contour.

The rationalization of $\alpha-\lambda$ in equation (12) is exact. Near
zero it gives a uniform quadratic lower bound; at large imaginary part it
gives square-root growth. Both are sufficient for an integrable absolute
envelope. In equation (14), all remaining correction terms have an exponential
factor in the horizon, while the drift contributes at most a factor of order
$T$. The apparently unbounded factor $|\sqrt{2s}|$ is absorbed by its
exponential decay on the same contour. Thus the whole-contour correction is
uniformly negligible.

The local radius $T^{-2/5}$ gives $T\delta_T^3\to0$ and
$\sqrt T\delta_T\to\infty$, so the Taylor remainder and omitted Gaussian
tails are both controlled. Re-evaluating the Gaussian integral gives exactly
the prefactor $1/(s_T\sqrt{\pi T v_T})$. The prefactor is the same in
the numerator and denominator because both use the common horizon $T$ and
common $\mu_T$; the $T-t$ difference is explicitly in the amplitude.

## Limit normalization and path-space scope

Taking the ratio of the two saddle expansions gives equation (20), with
quadratic killing $\lambda_r^2/2$. Direct application of Itô's formula
to $e^{-\lambda_r W_t^2/2}$ produces the same density. The proposed local
martingale is bounded on every fixed time interval, so its expectation is one
without an unproved Novikov condition. Its transformed drift is
$-\lambda_r x$.

Pointwise convergence of nonnegative densities, each integrating to one,
then gives total variation convergence through Scheffé's lemma. Applying the
saddle lemma path by path is valid: at fixed $t$, both the endpoint and the
already-spent budget are finite. The uniform-on-compact lemma therefore
supplies pointwise convergence for every such path. The argument for weak
convergence on $C([0,\infty))$ using constant extensions and bounded
Lipschitz functions is also valid; it does not interchange an uncontrolled
large observation time with the conditioning horizon.
