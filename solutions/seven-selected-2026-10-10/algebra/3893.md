# Problem 3893: simultaneous kernel and cokernel for whiskered graphs

**Result:** a proof of the conjecture over every field. If a graph $G$
on $n$ vertices has an independent set of size three, then the map
from degree $\lceil n/2\rceil$ to degree $\lceil n/2\rceil+1$ fails
maximal rank for every linear form in the associated algebra.

## Source and precise statement

The source is Holleben--Nicklasson, *Roller Coaster Gorenstein algebras
and Koszul algebras failing the weak Lefschetz property*, Journal of
Pure and Applied Algebra **230** (2026), 108238. Conjecture 1.3 appears
on page 3 and is repeated as Conjecture 3.15 on page 10. It concerns
whiskering at **every** vertex and adjoining the squares of **all**
vertex variables.

- [Final published text](https://mdu.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:2053155/FULLTEXT01.pdf).
- [Author preprint, Conjectures 1.3 and 3.15](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00155v2).

Write $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ for the original vertices and $y_1,\ldots,y_n$
for their whiskers. The algebra is

$$
A=K[x_1,\ldots,x_n,y_1,\ldots,y_n]/J,
$$

where

$$
J=(x_i^2,y_i^2,x_i y_i:1\le i\le n)
 +(x_i x_j:\{i,j\}\in E(G)).
$$

All variables have degree one. The weak Lefschetz property (WLP) means
that some $\ell\in A_1$ makes every multiplication map
$A_j\to A_{j+1}$ have maximal rank.

## Two invariant summands prove failure

Put

$$
L=\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i+y_i),\qquad Y=\sum_{i=1}^n y_i.
$$

The substitution

$$
T(x_i)=x_i,\qquad T(y_i)=y_i-x_i
$$

induces a graded algebra automorphism of $A$ and satisfies $T(L)=Y$.
Indeed, it preserves each original-edge relation, and

$$
T(x_i y_i)=x_i y_i-x_i^2=0,\qquad
T(y_i^2)=y_i^2-2x_i y_i+x_i^2=0.
$$

The inverse substitution is $y_i\mapsto y_i+x_i$. These identities
hold in every characteristic.

Let $\operatorname{Ind}(G)$ denote all independent vertex sets,
including the empty set, and write $x_C=\prod_{i\in C}x_i$. The
standard monomial basis gives a direct sum

$$
A=\bigoplus_{C\in\operatorname{Ind}(G)}V_C,\qquad
V_C=x_C\,
K[y_j:j\notin C]/(y_j^2:j\notin C).
$$

Here $V_C$ is a vector subspace, with degrees shifted by $|C|$.
Multiplication by $Y$ preserves **each** $V_C$: a term $y_i$ with
$i\in C$ kills $x_C$, while every other term only changes the
whisker subset. Thus the multiplication map is a direct sum of maps
on these subspaces.

Set $d=\lceil n/2\rceil$. In the empty-set summand,

$$
\dim(V_\varnothing)_d=\binom nd
>\binom n{d+1}=\dim(V_\varnothing)_{d+1}.
$$

This block necessarily has a nonzero kernel.

Choose an independent triple $C$. In its summand,

$$
\dim(V_C)_d=\binom{n-3}{d-3}
<\binom{n-3}{d-2}=\dim(V_C)_{d+1}.
$$

This block necessarily has a nonzero cokernel. As usual,
$\binom ab=0$ for $b<0$ or $b>a$. For $n=3,4$, the last
inequality is $0<1$. For $n\ge5$, it follows from

$$
\frac{\binom{n-3}{d-2}}{\binom{n-3}{d-3}}
=\frac{n-d}{d-2}>1,
$$

because $2d<n+2$.

The direct sum map $Y:A_d\to A_{d+1}$ consequently has both a
nonzero kernel and a nonzero cokernel. It has rank strictly smaller
than both dimensions, regardless of which dimension is larger.
Conjugation by $T$ gives the same conclusion for $L$.

More quantitatively, if $i_3(G)$ counts independent triples, then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dim\ker(L:A_d\to A_{d+1})
&\ge\binom nd-\binom n{d+1}>0,\\
\dim\operatorname{coker}(L:A_d\to A_{d+1})
&\ge i_3(G)\left[\binom{n-3}{d-2}-\binom{n-3}{d-3}\right]>0.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Why every linear form fails

For a monomial ideal, diagonal rescaling of variables is an algebra
automorphism. Thus every linear form with all $2n$ coefficients
nonzero has the same multiplication rank as $L$.

To include forms with zero coefficients and arbitrary ground fields,
extend scalars to an algebraic closure $\overline K$. Ranks of
matrices defined over $K$ are unchanged by this extension. In the
coefficient space $\overline K^{2n}$, existence of a nonzero maximal
minor of the degree-$d$ multiplication matrix is an open condition.
If any form had maximal rank in that degree, this nonempty open set
would intersect the dense open set where all coefficients are
nonzero. That contradicts the failure just proved for every form in
that latter set.

Therefore every linear form fails maximal rank in degree $d$, and
$A$ does not have the WLP. This proves the catalog's conjecture.

## Explicit certificates in the original variables

There is also a direct way to exhibit both rank defects without
computing ranks. Put $z_i=x_i+y_i$, so $z_i^2=0$ in $A$.

For $n=2r$, define

$$
P=\prod_{j=1}^{r}(z_{2j-1}-z_{2j}).
$$

For $n=2r+1$, use

$$
P=z_n\prod_{j=1}^{r}(z_{2j-1}-z_{2j}).
$$

In either case $P\in A_d$ and $LP=0$, by
$(z_a+z_b)(z_a-z_b)=0$ and $z_n^2=0$. The expansion of $P$
contains an all-whisker monomial with coefficient $1$ or $-1$,
and every all-whisker monomial survives the quotient. Thus $P\ne0$
over every field.

For a cokernel certificate, relabel an independent triple as
$C=\{n-2,n-1,n\}$. Put $m=n-3$ and

$$
Q=\prod_{j=1}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}(y_{2j-1}-y_{2j})
\prod_{i=n-2}^{n}(x_i-y_i).
$$

This is the independent-set construction in the source's Theorem
3.10. Its degree is
$3+\lfloor(n-3)/2\rfloor=d+1$. Every expanded monomial survives in
$A$, because the selected original vertices lie in $C$, and no
term contains both $x_i$ and $y_i$.

Use the surviving squarefree monomials as orthonormal coordinate
bases, meaning that coefficient pairing identifies vectors and
covectors. The transpose of multiplication by $L$ removes one
variable from each monomial. Hence it acts on these coordinate
polynomials as

$$
D=\sum_i\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}
+\frac{\partial}{\partial y_i}\right).
$$

This is a statement about the multiplication matrix and its
transpose; it does not assert that differentiation descends to the
quotient as an algebra derivation. Pairwise cancellation gives
$DQ=0$, and $Q\ne0$. Thus its coefficient covector annihilates
$L A_d$, certifying a nonzero cokernel. Combined with $P$, this
also proves the rank defect directly.
