# Problem 212: a counterexample over the affine cusp

**Result:** negative answer to the catalog's question for locally finite,
nonnegatively graded algebras. The example has a semisimple degree-zero
part. Its grading is not standard, so it does not settle the additional
standard-grading restriction in Dramburg's later Question 6.2.

## Source and scope

Dramburg's thesis, Section 2.5.3, printed page 31, asks whether every
complete set of primitive orthogonal idempotents in a locally finite
nonnegatively graded algebra can be sent to homogeneous idempotents by an
automorphism. Dramburg--Sandøy, Question 3.10, asks for the degree-zero
parts specifically. The September 2026 follow-up, Question 6.2, retains
the general question as well as a more restrictive version.

- [Thesis, Section 2.5.3](https://uu.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1954180/FULLTEXT01.pdf).
- [Dramburg--Sandøy, Question 3.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.13283).
- [Dramburg, Question 6.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03288v1#S6).

The argument below rules out **every** algebra automorphism and **every**
homogeneous target, not merely conjugation by a unit or the particular
degree-zero target.

## The example

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, put

$$
B=k[t],\qquad R=k[t^2,t^3]=k\oplus t^2k[t],\qquad A=M_2(R).
$$

Give $R$ its grading induced from $\deg t=1$, and give the matrix units
degree zero. Thus

$$
A_0=M_2(k),\qquad A_1=0,\qquad A_d=t^dM_2(k)\quad(d\ge2).
$$

This is a unital nonnegative grading and every component has dimension
at most four over $k$.

In $M_2(B)$, set

$$
S=\begin{pmatrix}
1+t&t^2\\
-t^2&1-t+t^2-t^3
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The identity

$$
(1+t)(1-t+t^2-t^3)+t^4=1
$$

gives $\det S=1$. Define

$$
e=SE_{11}S^{-1}
=\begin{pmatrix}
1-t^4&-t^2-t^3\\
-t^2+t^3-t^4+t^5&t^4
\end{pmatrix},\qquad f=1-e.
$$

All entries of $e$ and $f$ lie in $R$. They are nonzero orthogonal
idempotents with $e+f=1$, and their degree-zero parts are $E_{11}$ and
$E_{22}$, respectively.

## A nonfree cross-corner

For $a\in k$, introduce the $R$-submodule

$$
L_a=\{u\in k[t]:u'(0)=a u(0)\}.
$$

It is an $R$-module because $r'(0)=0$ for every $r\in R$.

**Lemma.** If $a\ne0$, then $L_a$ is not a free $R$-module of rank
one.

**Proof.** It contains $1+at$ and $t^2$. These polynomials are
coprime in $B$, so the ideal $B L_a$ generated by $L_a$ in $B$ is
$B$. If $L_a=Ru$ were free of rank one, extension of this equality
inside $B$ would give $B=Bu$. Therefore $u$ would be a nonzero
constant. Such a constant does not belong to $L_a$ when $a\ne0$.
This is a contradiction. □

Now compute the $R$-module $eAf$. In $M_2(B)$, every element of
$eM_2(B)f$ has a unique expression

$$
S(uE_{12})S^{-1}\qquad(u\in B).
$$

The equality $eAf=eM_2(B)f\cap M_2(R)$ holds: any matrix $X$ in
the intersection satisfies $eXf=X$, so it already belongs to $eAf$.

Reduction modulo $t^2B$ gives

$$
S\equiv\operatorname{diag}(1+t,1-t),\qquad
S^{-1}\equiv\operatorname{diag}(1-t,1+t).
$$

Consequently

$$
S(uE_{12})S^{-1}\equiv (1+2t)uE_{12}\pmod{t^2M_2(B)}.
$$

A polynomial belongs to $R$ exactly when its coefficient of $t$
vanishes. Thus this matrix belongs to $M_2(R)$ exactly when

$$
u'(0)+2u(0)=0.
$$

The map $u\mapsto S(uE_{12})S^{-1}$ therefore restricts to an
$R$-linear isomorphism

$$
eAf\cong L_{-2}.
$$

Since $2\ne0$ in $k$, the lemma shows that $eAf$ is not free of
rank one over the center $R$.

## Primitivity

The same calculation with $E_{11}$ in place of $E_{12}$ gives

$$
S(uE_{11})S^{-1}\equiv uE_{11}\pmod{t^2M_2(B)}.
$$

Its entries belong to $R$ exactly when $u\in R$. Hence

$$
eAe\cong R.
$$

Likewise $fAf\cong R$. Since $R\subset k[t]$ is a domain, these
corner rings have no idempotents other than zero and their identities.
Thus both $e$ and $f$ are primitive in $A$, and $\{e,f\}$ is a
complete set of primitive orthogonal idempotents.

## Obstruction to every algebra automorphism

A nonzero homogeneous idempotent in a nonnegative grading has degree
zero: its square has twice its degree. Therefore a homogeneous
primitive idempotent $h\in A$ belongs to $M_2(k)$. It has matrix
rank one, and constant conjugation sends it to $E_{11}$. It follows
that

$$
hA(1-h)\cong E_{11}M_2(R)E_{22}\cong R
$$

as a module over the center $Z(A)=R\cdot1$.

Suppose that an algebra automorphism $\varphi:A\to A$ sent $e$ to such
an $h$. Its restriction to the center is a ring automorphism
$\sigma:R\to R$, and $\varphi$ induces a bijection

$$
eAf\longrightarrow hA(1-h),\qquad
\varphi(rx)=\sigma(r)\varphi(x).
$$

Semilinear bijections with an automorphism of the coefficient ring
preserve being free of rank one. Explicitly, if $y$ is a basis of the
target and $x=\varphi^{-1}(y)$, every element $z$ of the source can
be uniquely written as $\sigma^{-1}(r)x$, where
$\varphi(z)=ry$. This would make $eAf$ free of rank one over $R$,
contrary to the cross-corner calculation.

No such automorphism exists. In particular, no automorphism can send
the set $\{e,f\}$ to any complete set of homogeneous idempotents.

## Verification and limits

Run the independent exact polynomial calculations with

```sh
python solutions/seven-selected-2026-10-10/algebra/verify_212.py
```

The script uses integer polynomial arithmetic to verify $\det S=1$,
the displayed matrix for $e$, orthogonality, the absence of linear
terms, and the reductions modulo $t^2$ that identify the corner
modules. Nonfreeness and the obstruction to arbitrary automorphisms are
proved above; finite numerical testing is not used for those claims.

The example works over every field of characteristic different from
two. Characteristic zero supplies an instance within all the stated
source hypotheses. The grading has $A_1=0$, so it does **not** answer
the standard-grading variant of the question.
