**Date:** 2026-10-10.

**Reviewed files:** `../discrete/README.md`, `../discrete/Q3840.md`,

**Conclusion:** the circumference-five theorems and positive covering

No blocking mathematical or implementation issue was found.

## Source and scope

The publisher's [full primary article](https://combinatorialpress.com/jcmcc-articles/volume-129/from-princes-on-chessboards-to-proper-total-domination-in-graphs/)
was independently inspected. Problem 2.8 asks about odd $n\geq5$ and
$m\geq6$ outside the residue class $3$ modulo $4$. Section 4(II) asks
about products of two odd cycles with both circumferences at least five.
The neighbor-count definition matches the verifier: every count is
positive, and counts at endpoints of every edge are unequal.

The submitted restricted theorems are:

* $C_5\square P_m$ admits a set precisely when
  $m\in\{3,5,7\}\cup\{9,10,11,\ldots\}$.
* For odd $m\geq5$, $C_5\square C_m$ admits a set precisely when
  $m\geq7$.

These answer nontrivial slices of the primary questions. The statements
about larger circumferences divisible by five are sufficient conditions.
They do not assert that nonexistence survives passage to a covering.

## Re-derivation of the transfer equivalence

For successive membership rows $a,b,c$, the count at coordinate $i$ of
the middle row is exactly

$$
s_i=b_{i-1}+b_{i+1}+a_i+c_i,
$$

with horizontal indices reduced modulo five. Requiring positivity and
$s_i\ne s_{i+1}$ checks every horizontal edge. For an overlapping next
triple $(b,c,d)$, coordinatewise inequality of its count vector with
$s$ checks every vertical edge joining the middle rows. Thus the local
state and transition conditions are jointly necessary and sufficient.

A cylinder uses two all-zero exterior membership rows. A walk of
$m$ states finalizes its $m$ actual rows, so the recurrence starts at
$m=1$, not $m=0$. At $m=1$ the single state must satisfy both exterior
conditions. This explains the indexing in the code.

A torus requires a closed walk of length $m$. The overlap equalities
close the membership word as well as the row-count constraints. Repeated
states and repeated arcs are legitimate. Restricting to simple directed
cycles would be incorrect; the implementation explicitly avoids this.
For $m\geq3$ the two vertical neighbors are distinct, as required for
the simple Cartesian-product graph.

## Independent finite checks

The separate script [review_discrete_checks.py](review_discrete_checks.py)
does not import the original verifier. It uses explicit membership words
to check the only negative torus instance needed by the theorem.

It enumerates $4,310$ three-row words whose middle row is valid. Extending
while finalizing each additional middle row leaves $10,800$ four-row
words and $25,170$ five-row words. For each five-row word, it then checks
the first and last rows directly using cyclic neighbors, plus the three
vertical edge sets omitted during linear extension. **Zero** words pass.
This independently proves that $C_5\square C_5$ has no proper total
dominating set.

A separate direct frontier recurrence agrees with the original
cylinder counts through $m=8$:

| Path length | Frontier size | Accepting frontiers |

| 1 | 60 | 0 |
| 2 | 160 | 0 |
| 3 | 350 | 10 |

| 5 | 770 | 20 |

This establishes all five negative path lengths in the asserted full
circumference-five classification. The entries count frontiers, not
membership sets. No floating-point optimization, timeout, or guessed
pattern is used.

The original verifier was also rerun. It checks the supplied seeds
against graph neighborhoods separately from its transfer routine and
compares the actual reachable sets at lengths 15 and 16 before claiming
stabilization. Equal cardinalities alone would not suffice.

## Re-derivation of insertion and lifting

For $C=10010$ and $D=11011$, direct addition gives

$$
T(C,D,C)=(4,1,2,3,2),\qquad
T(D,C,D)=(2,3,1,2,4).
$$

Each vector is positive and proper around the horizontal cycle,
including its last-to-first edge. The vectors differ at every
coordinate. At a $C,D,C$ occurrence, inserting $(C,D)^r$ after the
middle $D$ preserves all original row-count vectors. The first new
edge compares the displayed vectors; the final new edge has the same
count vectors as the original $D,C$ edge. Every internal inserted
edge alternates the displayed vectors. This proves the insertion for
every $r\geq0$, including the torus occurrence across the row origin.

The path seeds at lengths 7 and 12 consequently supply all lengths
$7+2r$ and $12+2r$. With the lengths 3, 5, and 10, they supply exactly
the asserted positive set. The torus seed at length 13 supplies every
odd length at least 13; the seeds at 7, 9, and 11 complete the claim.
This is an infinite construction, not an extrapolation of finite data.

Finally, reducing a cycle coordinate modulo five is bijective on each
vertex neighborhood. For a pulled-back selected set, all neighbor
counts are therefore inherited exactly, and adjacent images remain
adjacent. This proves the covering extensions. It provides no converse,
as the submitted files correctly state.
