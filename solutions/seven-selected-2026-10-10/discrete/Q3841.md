# Q3841: odd tori with one circumference equal to five

**Result type:** computer-assisted partial theorem, with constructive infinite families. **Catalog status: `partial`; the general question remains unresolved.**

The primary formulation is Osborn–Zhang, *From princes on chessboards to proper total domination in graphs*, the question concluding Section 3 and repeated in Section 4(II), p. 46 ([published version](https://doi.org/10.61091/jcmcc129-03)). It asks which products of two odd cycles, each of length at least five, admit a proper total dominating set.

## Theorem

For every odd integer `m >= 5`,

$$
 C_5\square C_m\text{ has a proper total dominating set}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\ge7.
$$

Consequently, for every odd positive integer `k` and every odd `m>=7`, the graph `C_(5k) □ C_m` has a proper total dominating set. By interchanging factors, the same holds for `C_m □ C_(5k)`. These covering statements are sufficient conditions only.

## Exhaustive nonexistence certificate at `m=5`

The exact transfer lemma in [README.md](README.md) identifies membership patterns on `C_5 □ C_m` with closed walks of length `m` in the 4,310-state, 10,800-arc directed graph defined there. Repeated states and arcs are permitted. This qualification is necessary: searching only simple directed cycles would not decide torus existence.

For every possible starting state `s`, [verify.py](verify.py) initializes `A_0(s)={s}` and performs the exact recurrence

$$
 A_{j+1}(s)=\bigcup_{u\in A_j(s)}N^+(u).
$$

A closed walk of length `j` from `s` exists exactly when `s` belongs to `A_j(s)`. The enumeration obtains:

| Walk length `j` | Number of states `s` with `s in A_j(s)` |
| ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 0 |
| 2 | 20 |
| 3 | 0 |
| 4 | 60 |
| 5 | 0 |

The zero at length 5 proves that `C_5 □ C_5` has no proper total dominating set. The walk counts at lengths 1 and 2 are automaton data only; they are not claims about simple cycle graphs of those lengths. The length-3 zero is a consistency check outside Q3841's range.

The selected-set sizes are respectively `21,26,33,36`. These are feasible sizes, with no minimality claim.

In the `m=13` column the last row, first row, and second row are `C,D,C`, where `C=10010` and `D=11011`. Insert `r` copies of the pair `C,D` immediately after its first row. The insertion lemma in [README.md](README.md) gives a valid construction of length

$$
 m=13+2r\qquad(r\ge0).
$$

It uses the two exact neighbor-count vectors

$$
 T(D,C,D)=(2,3,1,2,4),\qquad T(C,D,C)=(4,1,2,3,2),
$$

which are positive, proper around the horizontal 5-cycle, and different coordinatewise. The insertion preserves all old vectors and the wraparound boundary. Thus the construction holds for every odd `m>=13`. Together with the three smaller seeds it covers every odd `m>=7`. Combining this with the length-5 nonexistence certificate proves the theorem. ∎

## Covering extension and remaining question

Reduction of the first cycle coordinate modulo five is a graph covering. The pullback of a selected set preserves each neighbor count exactly, by the covering lemma in [README.md](README.md). This proves the stated families at odd circumferences divisible by five.

The theorem and symmetry settle every case of Q3841 with one circumference equal to five. They do not classify all pairs of odd circumferences at least seven. No assertion is made that failure at `C_5 □ C_5` persists under covers. Q3841 therefore uses status `partial`; its general question remains unresolved.
