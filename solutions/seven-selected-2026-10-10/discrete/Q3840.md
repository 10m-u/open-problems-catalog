# Q3840: a complete circumference-five classification

**Result type:** computer-assisted partial theorem, with constructive infinite families. **Catalog status: `partial`; the general question remains unresolved.**

The primary formulation is Osborn–Zhang, *From princes on chessboards to proper total domination in graphs*, Problem 2.8, p. 39 ([published version](https://doi.org/10.61091/jcmcc129-03)). It asks for an odd-cycle cylinder classification. The paper supplies the residue class `m = 3 mod 4` and the small `C_5 □ P_5` example. The result below goes beyond those cases.

## Theorem

For every integer `m >= 1`,

$$
 C_5\square P_m\text{ has a proper total dominating set}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 m\in\{3,5,7\}\cup\{9,10,11,\ldots\}.
$$

In particular, within Q3840's stated range `m >= 6`, `m != 3 mod 4`, the only failures at circumference five are `m=6` and `m=8`.

For every odd positive integer `k`, the graph `C_(5k) □ P_m` has such a set whenever `m` belongs to the displayed positive set. This last assertion is a sufficient family, not an if-and-only-if statement for `k>1`.

## Exhaustive proof of the negative cases

Use the transfer graph and transfer lemma in [README.md](README.md). Let `R_1` consist of its states with first row `00000`. Define

$$
 R_{m+1}=\{v:\exists u\in R_m,\ u\longrightarrow v\},
 \qquad
 F=\{(a,b,00000): (a,b,00000)\text{ is legal}\}.
$$

The transfer lemma gives an equivalence, not merely a necessary condition:

$$
 C_5\square P_m\text{ admits a set}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R_m\cap F\ne\varnothing.
$$

The exact enumeration in `cylinder_certificate` in [verify.py](verify.py) gives:

| `m` | Size of `R_m` | Size of `R_m intersect F` |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 60 | 0 |
| 2 | 160 | 0 |
| 3 | 350 | 10 |
| 4 | 480 | 0 |
| 5 | 770 | 20 |
| 6 | 1150 | 0 |
| 7 | 1710 | 50 |
| 8 | 1910 | 0 |

Consequently no set exists for `m=1,2,4,6,8`. These numbers count reachable states and accepting states, not the number of selected vertex sets. All `32768` candidate triples and all compatible arcs are checked; the nonexistence claims do not rely on heuristic search.

The seeds contain respectively `10,14,20,32,36` selected vertices. These sizes certify constructions and are not claimed to be minimum domination numbers.

Both the `m=7` and `m=12` columns contain `C,D,C` in rows 3–5, where `C=10010` and `D=11011`. The two-row insertion lemma therefore supplies:

$$
 m=7+2r\quad\text{and}\quad m=12+2r,\qquad r\ge0.
$$

Together with the `m=3,5,10` seeds, these are every positive integer except `1,2,4,6,8`. This proves the existence direction for every `m`; it is not an extrapolation from a finite sample. The negative cases above establish the converse. ∎

## A second certificate for the infinite tail

The same exact enumeration reaches 3,460 states at each of `m=15` and `m=16`, with 50 accepting states. The verifier checks **equality of the two actual state sets**, not merely equality of their cardinalities. The recurrence then implies `R_m=R_15` for every `m>=15`. All intermediate positive lengths 9–14 are explicitly checked in [verification.json](verification.json). This is an independent finite-state proof of the positive infinite tail, in addition to the explicit insertion construction.

## What remains

The theorem resolves the circumference-five slice and supplies positive covering families at all odd circumferences divisible by five. It does not characterize cylinders of every odd circumference `n>=7`, and it does not exclude additional sets in covers of the negative cases. Q3840 therefore uses status `partial`; its general question remains unresolved.
